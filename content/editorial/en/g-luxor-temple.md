Luxor Temple stands in the heart of modern Luxor, on the East Bank of the Nile. The ancient Egyptians called it *Ipet-resyt*, the "Southern Sanctuary", because of its place in ancient Thebes. This guide explains what you will see, using the facts on the page of Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities.

## A temple that faces Karnak {#karnak}

Luxor Temple lies about three kilometres south of Karnak Temple. A processional way bordered with sphinxes once linked the two. The oldest evidence for the temple dates to the Eighteenth Dynasty (about 1550–1295 BC).

Unlike most ancient Egyptian temples, it is not laid out on an east–west axis: it is turned towards Karnak. The reason is the Opet Festival, one of the most important religious celebrations of ancient Egypt. The cult images of Amun, his wife Mut and their son, the moon god Khonsu, were carried from their temples at Karnak in a grand procession to Luxor Temple, to visit the god who lived there, Amenemopet.

## Who built it {#builders}

The temple was not the work of one ruler:

- **Hatshepsut** (about 1473–1458 BC): the oldest surviving structure, a shrine.
- **Amenhotep III** (about 1390–1352 BC): the core of the temple, with a columned hall and, in front of it, a courtyard with columns around its sides. He also built the Great Colonnade.
- **Tutankhamun** (about 1336–1327 BC) and **Horemheb** (about 1323–1295 BC): finished the decoration of the Great Colonnade, including its scenes of the Opet Festival.
- **Ramesses II** (about 1279–1213 BC): many additions at the front of the temple.

The temple is part of Ancient Thebes with its Necropolis, a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1979.

## Inside the temple {#inside}

From the entrance, you walk back through time:

1. **The pylon of Ramesses II:** a gate with two towers, the entrance to the temple. Colossal statues stand in front of it, with one of a pair of obelisks about 25 metres high. The other obelisk has stood in the Place de la Concorde in Paris since 1836.
2. **The peristyle courtyard of Ramesses II:** built in front of the Great Colonnade.
3. **The Great Colonnade of Amenhotep III:** two rows of seven colossal columns, with the Opet Festival scenes on its walls.
4. **The courtyard and hall of Amenhotep III:** the core of the temple.
5. **The Divine Birth room:** one of the inner rooms has a series of scenes known as the Divine Birth. They tell how the king's true father was the god Amun-Ra himself, disguised as Thutmose IV (about 1400–1390 BC).

## The Roman paintings {#roman}

In the late third century AD, the Romans built a fort around the temple. The first room beyond Amenhotep III's hypostyle hall became its sanctuary. Its old reliefs were covered with plaster and painted in the Graeco-Roman style, showing Emperor Diocletian (284–305 AD) and his three co-rulers. Much of the painting had faded, and work is under way to restore it.

## How to visit {#visiting}

- **On the East Bank:** Luxor Temple is in the centre of town, and many visitors see it on the same day as Karnak. Our guide to [Karnak and Luxor Temple](/guides/karnak-and-luxor-temple/) helps you plan that day.
- **Tickets and opening hours:** these change, so check the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities' website before you go.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? Who built Luxor Temple?
Several kings. The oldest surviving part is a shrine of Hatshepsut; the core is the work of Amenhotep III; Tutankhamun and Horemheb finished the colonnade's decoration; and Ramesses II added the courtyard, the pylon and the obelisks in front.

?? Why does Luxor Temple face Karnak?
Because it was the main stage of the Opet Festival, when the images of Amun, Mut and Khonsu were carried in procession from Karnak to Luxor Temple.

?? Where is the other obelisk of Luxor Temple?
In the Place de la Concorde in Paris, since 1836. Its twin, about 25 metres high, still stands in front of the pylon.

?? How far is Luxor Temple from Karnak?
About three kilometres. A processional way bordered with sphinxes once linked the two temples.
