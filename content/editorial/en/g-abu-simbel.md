Abu Simbel is the one day of an Egypt trip that most people plan around. The two rock temples of Ramesses II stand 280 km south of Aswan, near the Sudanese border, so the visit is a full day away from the city. This guide covers what you will see, how the temples were saved, the ways to get there, and how to choose between the tours on offer.

## What you will see {#what-you-will-see}

### The Great Temple
Carved out of a sandstone cliff on the west bank of the Nile, the Great Temple is fronted by four seated statues of Ramesses II, each about 20 metres high, two on either side of the entrance. Small figures of his queen Nefertari, his mother and his children stand around their feet. Inside, three halls run some 56 metres into the rock, lined with statues of the king in the form of Osiris and with scenes of his campaign at the Battle of Kadesh. The temple is dedicated to the sun gods Amun-Ra and Ra-Horakhty.

### The Small Temple
Just to the north stands a smaller temple, dedicated to Queen Nefertari for the worship of the goddess Hathor. Its façade carries standing statues of the king and the queen, about 10.5 metres high.

> **Before you go in**
> On the southern colossi, look for graffiti left by Greek mercenaries serving Egypt in the 6th century BC. They are among the early evidence for the Greek alphabet.

## How the temples were saved {#rescue}

When the Aswan High Dam was built, the rising waters of Lake Nasser threatened to submerge Abu Simbel. Between 1963 and 1968, an international team, funded by more than 50 countries, cut away the top of the cliff, took both temples apart and rebuilt them on higher ground, about 60 metres above their original site. It was part of the UNESCO campaign of 1960 to 1980 that moved the temples of Nubia, and Abu Simbel is now part of the World Heritage site *Nubian Monuments from Abu Simbel to Philae*, inscribed in 1979.

## Getting there: three ways {#getting-there}

- **By road from Aswan.** The usual choice. Tours leave Aswan before dawn and return in the afternoon, so most of the day is spent in the vehicle. Most include hotel pickup.
- **By air.** Short flights from Aswan save the long drive, at a higher cost. Check the flight times against the temple visit you want.
- **By Lake Nasser cruise.** Cruises on Lake Nasser between Aswan and Abu Simbel reach the temples by water. Other temples moved during the rescue, such as Wadi es-Sebua and Amada, stand by the lake.

## Choosing a tour {#choosing-a-tour}

Day trips from Aswan come in two main forms:

- **Private tours.** A car and driver for you alone, often with an Egyptologist guide. You agree the pickup time and the pace with the driver.
- **Shared tours.** You join a group in a minibus or coach. They are cheaper and the route is the same, but pickups and timings are fixed.

> **What to check before you book**
> - Whether the temple entry tickets are included or paid on the day.
> - Whether a guide comes into the temples or only drives.
> - The pickup time and place, and how long you will have at the site.
> - The cancellation terms, especially around the sun days.

## The sun days in February and October {#sun-days}

On two days of the year, about **22 February** and **22 October**, the first rays of the morning sun run the full length of the Great Temple and light the statues in the innermost sanctuary. It still happens today, on the rebuilt temple. These mornings bring large crowds and special events, so book well ahead and expect a very early start. On other days you will have more space inside the temples.

## Practical tips {#tips}

- Take water, a snack and sun protection. The forecourt has little shade.
- Carry a layer for the pre-dawn drive. Desert mornings can be cool in winter.
- Photography rules inside the temples can change. Follow the guards.
- Leave the rest of the day free. Plan Philae, a felucca and the Nubian villages in [Aswan](/destinations/aswan/) for the days before or after.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? How far is Abu Simbel from Aswan?
About 280 km to the south, near the border with Sudan. Most visitors go on a day trip by road or take a short flight.

?? Is a private tour worth it?
If you want a guide inside the temples and a flexible pace, yes. A shared tour sees the same temples for less, with fixed timings.

?? When does the sun reach the sanctuary?
On or around 22 February and 22 October, at sunrise. On those days the temples are at their busiest.

?? Can I see both temples on a day trip?
Yes. The Great Temple and the Small Temple stand next to each other, and day trips include both.
