## What it is {#what-it-is}

A private tour of the Giza plateau with your own Egyptologist guide and driver. You'll see the three great pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, the panoramic viewpoint and the Sphinx with its valley temple, at your own pace and without joining a group. The plateau is part of the UNESCO World Heritage site *Memphis and its Necropolis — the Pyramid Fields from Giza to Dahshur*.

## Why a guide makes a difference here {#why-a-guide}

Giza is simple to reach but surprisingly hard to do well. The site is large and exposed, the approaches are crowded with touts, and the story behind the monuments doesn't explain itself: how the pyramids were built, what the causeways and temples were for, why the Sphinx faces east. A good guide:

- handles tickets and the route across the plateau;
- keeps unwanted sellers at a distance;
- turns the stones into the history of the Old Kingdom.

## What you actually do {#what-you-do}

- Walk around the Great Pyramid, the only surviving Wonder of the Ancient World.
- Visit the pyramids of Khafre and Menkaure and the smaller queens' pyramids.
- Stop at the panoramic viewpoint for the classic photo of all the pyramids in a row.
- Descend to the Sphinx and walk through the valley temple beside it.
- Optionally, go inside one of the pyramids. This needs an extra ticket; the passages are narrow, steep and hot.

## Who it suits {#who-it-suits}

First-time visitors who want context and a hassle-free morning, families, and anyone short on time. Independent travellers who like to read up beforehand can visit alone, but should expect persistent offers from touts.

## Before you go {#before-you-go}

> **Practical notes**
> - **Timing:** book the earliest start you can. The light is softer and it's cooler and quieter.
> - **Duration:** a half-day is typical for the plateau alone.
> - **What to bring:** water, a hat, sun protection and comfortable shoes. There's very little shade.
> - **Inside the pyramids:** not recommended if you're claustrophobic or have mobility or heart problems.
> - **Camel and horse rides:** if you want one, agree the price, the duration and the route in full before you get on. A good guide will help.

## Combine it with {#combine}

- **The Grand Egyptian Museum**, right next to the plateau and home to the full Tutankhamun collection. Better as a separate half-day if you can.
- **Saqqara, Memphis and Dahshur** to the south: the Step Pyramid, and the quieter Bent and Red Pyramids.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? Is a private tour better than a group tour?
It's more flexible and calmer. You choose the pace and the stops. Group tours cost less but follow a fixed schedule.

?? Can I go inside the Great Pyramid?
Yes, with an extra ticket. The ascending passages are narrow and steep, and the chamber inside is bare. Most visitors find the outside and the viewpoint more impressive.

?? How early should I start?
As early as the site opens. Mornings are cooler and the crowds build through the day.
