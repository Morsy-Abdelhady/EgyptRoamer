Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities says the Museum of Islamic Art in Cairo holds one of the largest and most extensive collections of Islamic artefacts in the world. Its galleries take you through every period of Islamic history. This guide uses the facts on the Ministry's page.

## History {#history}

The idea of collecting and showing these objects began in 1880. The museum building opened in 1903, under Khedive Abbas Helmy II. Its façade is in the Mamluk style, and it stands next to the National Library of Egypt.

In 2014 a bomb aimed at the building opposite, the Cairo Security Directorate, damaged the museum's façade. The museum was restored and reopened in 2017.

## What you will see {#collection}

The museum has two floors. Its objects come from across the Islamic world and cover subjects such as astronomy, medicine and architecture. Among them:

- **Finely carved woodwork.**
- **Delicately decorated ceramics.**
- **Rare manuscripts.**

Two of the museum's most notable pieces are **a Mamluk key to the Ka'ba in Mecca** and **a textile with the oldest Kufic inscription**.

## Rules for visitors {#rules}

The Ministry's page lists the museum's rules. The main ones:

- **Photos:** photography with a mobile phone is free. No flash, and don't photograph other visitors or staff. Tripods and monopods are only for permitted commercial filming.
- **Bags:** large bags, backpacks and parcels bigger than 40 × 40 cm, umbrellas and sharp objects go in the cloakroom. Bags may be searched.
- **Food and drink:** not allowed in the galleries, except small bottles of water. No smoking anywhere in the museum.
- **Exhibits:** don't touch exhibits or showcases, and don't use flashlights or laser pointers.
- **Children** must stay with an adult at all times. No pets.

## Services {#services}

The Ministry lists a lecture room, a library, a lift, labels in Braille, toilets including an accessible toilet, parking, a ramped entrance, a gift shop and sign language.

## How to visit {#visiting}

- **Last tickets:** the ticket window closes an hour before the museum.
- **Tickets and opening hours:** these change, and Ramadan has its own hours, so check the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities' website before you go.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? When did the Museum of Islamic Art open?
In 1903, under Khedive Abbas Helmy II. The idea of the collection goes back to 1880.

?? Is the Museum of Islamic Art open after the 2014 explosion?
Yes. A 2014 bomb aimed at the Cairo Security Directorate opposite damaged its façade; the museum was restored and reopened in 2017.

?? What are the highlights of the Museum of Islamic Art?
Carved woodwork, decorated ceramics and rare manuscripts, with a Mamluk key to the Ka'ba in Mecca and a textile bearing the oldest Kufic inscription among its most notable pieces.

?? Can I take photos in the Museum of Islamic Art?
Yes: photography with a mobile phone is free. Flash is not allowed.
