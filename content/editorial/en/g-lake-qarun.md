Lake Qarun, in the Fayoum, is one of the oldest natural lakes in the world. Around it lie fossils from a time when this land was a river mouth by the sea, and monuments from Pharaonic and Roman times. This guide is based on the facts given by Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency, which manages the country's protected areas.

## The protected area in brief {#in-brief}

- **Declared:** 1989.
- **Area:** 1,298.3 km².
- **Type:** protected area for the sustainable use of natural resources.
- **Where:** Fayoum Governorate, about 90 km from Cairo.

## An ancient lake {#lake}

The lake is what remains of the ancient Lake Moeris. It is known worldwide for its fossil deposits from the sea, from rivers and from land, about 40 million years old.

## The fossils {#fossils}

A huge river mouth once lay here, and the Agency lists among the fossils found in the area:

- **The "Fayoum animal",** a giant creature that looked like a rhinoceros.
- **Ancestors of elephants and of the hippopotamus,** and **dolphins.**
- **Sharks** and **ancestors of African birds.**
- **Aegyptopithecus,** the oldest ape in the world, from the Oligocene.
- **Petrified trees.**

Today the area also has a wide variety of reptiles, amphibians and mammals.

## Antiquities around the lake {#antiquities}

North of the lake lie important historical sites from Roman and Pharaonic times. The Agency also names Qaret El-Rusas, northeast of the lake, and the area of the churches and the Monastery of Abu Lifa.

## How to visit {#visiting}

- **From Cairo:** about 90 km away.
- **Protect the place:** never take fossils, and leave no rubbish.
- **Entry fees and rules:** they can change, so check with the Environmental Affairs Agency or your operator before you go.
