Hurghada grew from a fishing village into the main resort city of Egypt's Red Sea coast. What draws people is underwater. Coral reefs start a short boat ride from shore, the water is clear, and diving and snorkelling are possible year-round. It isn't a historic town. It's a place for sea, sun and a break between temples, and it's close enough to Luxor to combine the two.

## Why go {#why-go}

- **Easy access to the reefs.** Dive and snorkel boats reach coral reefs and islands in a short trip.
- **Sea for every level.** Try-dives and snorkelling for beginners, and wrecks and drift dives for the experienced.
- **Warm all year.** Beach weather lasts well beyond the European summer.
- **A pairing with the Nile Valley.** [Luxor](/destinations/luxor/) is reachable by road, so temples and sea fit in one trip.

## Under the water {#underwater}

### The Giftun Islands
Protected islands offshore, with sandy beaches and fringing reefs. They're the classic day trip by boat for snorkelling and swimming.

### Diving
Hurghada's dive centres offer everything from first try-dives to multi-day liveaboards. Our [Red Sea diving trip](/experiences/red-sea-diving-two-reef-dives/) covers two reef dives from a boat with a guide.

### Snorkelling
Many reefs are shallow enough to be excellent with just a mask and fins, either on boat trips or from the house reefs of some resorts.

> **Reef etiquette**
> - Don't touch, stand on or collect coral or shells.
> - Don't feed fish.
> - Use reef-friendly sun protection, or better, a rash vest.
> - Choose operators who use fixed moorings instead of anchoring on the reef.

## Where to stay: the coast, area by area {#where-to-stay}

- **Hurghada town** (Sakkala, Dahar, the Marina): the busiest area, with restaurants, the marina and the most choice.
- **El Gouna:** a resort town to the north built around lagoons, popular for kitesurfing and with its own restaurants and nightlife.
- **Sahl Hasheesh, Makadi Bay and Soma Bay:** quieter resort bays to the south, mostly large all-inclusive hotels.

## Beyond the beach {#beyond-the-beach}

- **The desert.** Jeep and quad trips into the Eastern Desert mountains, often with a Bedouin-style dinner under the stars.
- **Kitesurfing and windsurfing.** The steady winds, especially around El Gouna and Soma Bay, make the coast a well-known kite spot.
- **Luxor day trips.** Possible by road, but it's a very long day. An overnight stay in Luxor is much better.

## How long, and when to go {#when-to-go}

**How long.** Three to five days is typical: enough for a boat trip, a few dives or snorkelling days, and rest.

**When to go.**
- **Year-round**, but spring and autumn give warm water without the peak summer heat.
- **Winter** is milder on land, with cooler water and more wind.
- **Summer** is hot, though sea breezes help.

## Getting there {#getting-there}

- Hurghada International Airport has direct flights from Cairo and many European cities.
- Roads connect the coast with Luxor and Cairo.
- Most resorts arrange transfers.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? Hurghada or Sharm El Sheikh?
Both are excellent for reefs. Hurghada combines more easily with [Luxor](/destinations/luxor/) and the Nile Valley. [Sharm El Sheikh](/destinations/sharm/) is closer to Ras Mohammed National Park and to Mount Sinai.

?? Can I snorkel if I can't swim well?
Many boat trips provide life jackets or snorkel vests, and guides accompany beginners. Tell the operator your level before you go.

?? Do I need a diving licence?
No, not for a try-dive with an instructor. For independent guided dives you need a recognised certification. Many centres run beginner courses.

?? How many days should I spend on the Red Sea?
Three days is enough for a taste. Five days or more if diving is a main reason for the trip.
