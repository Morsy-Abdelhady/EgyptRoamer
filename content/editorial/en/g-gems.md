Most first trips to Egypt follow the same route: Giza, Luxor, Aswan, maybe the Red Sea. That route is popular for good reason. But some of the country's most memorable places sit just off it, and they are often far quieter. These ten are all well-established sites, not secret spots. They're simply the ones that visitors in a hurry tend to leave out.

## Near Cairo {#near-cairo}

### 1. Dahshur: the Bent and Red Pyramids
South of Saqqara, two of the pharaoh Sneferu's pyramids stand in open desert. The Bent Pyramid changes angle halfway up, and you can go inside the Red Pyramid. They belong to the same UNESCO site as Giza, but they usually see a fraction of the visitors.

### 2. Wadi Al-Hitan (Whale Valley)
In the Fayoum desert, fossilised skeletons of early whales lie in the sand, with legs from the era when whales were evolving from land animals. It's Egypt's natural World Heritage site, and a surreal desert landscape. It needs a 4×4 and a long day from Cairo.

### 3. The Gayer-Anderson Museum and Ibn Tulun
In the middle of Islamic Cairo, a restored Ottoman-era house full of eclectic collections sits beside one of the city's oldest and most serene mosques.

## In the Nile Valley {#nile-valley}

### 4. Abydos: the Temple of Seti I
One of the most important religious sites of ancient Egypt, with some of the finest reliefs anywhere, and the famous list of kings. It's usually visited as a day trip from Luxor.

### 5. Dendera: the Temple of Hathor
A superbly preserved late temple, with painted ceilings and rooftop chapels. It's often combined with Abydos on the same day.

### 6. Medinet Habu
On Luxor's West Bank, the mortuary temple of Ramesses III has deeply carved, still-coloured reliefs. It's usually quieter than the Valley of the Kings.

### 7. Deir el-Medina
The village of the craftsmen who built the royal tombs, with their own small, brightly painted tombs. It's an intimate look at everyday life in ancient Thebes.

### 8. The Monastery of St Simeon, Aswan
The ruins of a fortified desert monastery on Aswan's west bank, reached by boat and a walk or camel ride through the sand.

## North and west {#north-and-west}

### 9. Rosetta (Rashid)
Where the Rosetta Stone was found, on the Nile's western mouth near Alexandria. Its Ottoman-era merchant houses are the attraction today.

### 10. Siwa Oasis
Remote, Amazigh, surrounded by salt lakes and dunes. See our full [Siwa guide](/destinations/siwa/).

> **Fitting them in**
> - From Cairo: Dahshur (half-day, easy to add to Saqqara); Wadi Al-Hitan (a long full day).
> - From Luxor: Abydos and Dendera (a full day together); Medinet Habu and Deir el-Medina (add to a West Bank day).
> - From Alexandria: Rosetta (a half or full day).
