Under a sheer wall of cliffs on Luxor's West Bank, three terraces rise one above the other. This is the temple of Hatshepsut, the queen who became pharaoh, and one of the most striking buildings in Egypt. This guide covers its story, what you will see and how to plan your visit.

## The queen who became pharaoh {#hatshepsut}

Hatshepsut ruled Egypt as pharaoh in about 1473–1458 BC. Her temple was called *Djeser-djeseru*, "the Holy of Holies", and was designed by Senenmut, the chief steward of the god Amun.

It was her mortuary temple, where her cult continued after her death. It also had places of worship for her father Thutmose I, for the goddess Hathor and for Anubis, the god of the dead, and an open-air altar for the sun god Ra-Horakhty. Pride of place went to Amun: his sanctuary is cut into the rock at the far end of the upper court.

## Why here {#deir-el-bahari}

The temple stands directly across the Nile from Karnak, the great temple of Amun. Each year, during a festival, the statues of Amun, his wife Mut and their son Khonsu crossed the river to visit the royal mortuary temples, and Hatshepsut's seems to have been one of their most important stops.

The cliffs were sacred to Hathor, who was believed to live in these hills; prayers show her as a cow coming out of the mountain. On the other side of the same hills lies the [Valley of the Kings](/guides/valley-of-the-kings-tombs-and-tickets/). About 600 years before Hatshepsut, King Nebhepetre Mentuhotep had already chosen this spot for his own tomb and temple, whose remains lie beside hers.

The Arabic name, Deir el-Bahari, means "the northern monastery", after a monastery built here in the 7th century. The temple was uncovered in 1894–96, and a Polish mission began restoring its upper terrace in 1968.

## What you'll see {#what-you-will-see}

- **The three terraces:** three man-made levels rise towards the cliff, each with a colonnade at its far end.
- **The Punt reliefs:** on the left side of the middle colonnade, Hatshepsut's expedition to the land of Punt, thought to be near modern Eritrea, with its people, their houses and the riches and exotic animals the Egyptians brought back.
- **The divine birth:** on the other side, Hatshepsut tells why she is the rightful king: her father Thutmose I named her as his heir, and her true father was the god Amun himself.
- **The obelisks:** other reliefs show temple rituals, festivals, and obelisks being carried from the quarry to Karnak.
- **The upper terrace:** statues of Hatshepsut as Osiris, the god of the dead, stand against the pillars in front of the open court.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? Who was Hatshepsut?
A queen who became pharaoh and ruled Egypt in about 1473–1458 BC.

?? What does Deir el-Bahari mean?
"The northern monastery", after a monastery built on the site in the 7th century.

?? What is the land of Punt?
A distant land reached by Hatshepsut's expedition, thought to be near modern Eritrea. The reliefs in her temple show its people, houses and animals.

?? Can I see the temple and the Valley of the Kings on the same day?
Yes. They lie on either side of the same hills, and the classic West Bank day includes both.
