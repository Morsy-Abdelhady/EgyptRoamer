The Valley of the Kings, on Luxor's West Bank, is where the pharaohs of Egypt's New Kingdom were buried. Walking down a corridor cut deep into the mountain, its walls still painted with gods and texts, is one of the great experiences of Egypt. This guide explains how the visit works.

## What the valley is {#what-it-is}

The valley was the burial site of almost all the kings of the 18th, 19th and 20th dynasties, from about 1539 to 1075 BC. To protect their rich burials, they hid their tombs in a lonely valley in the hills behind Deir el-Bahari. There are 62 known tombs. Most follow the same idea: a corridor descending into the rock, chambers along the way, and a burial chamber at the end, with walls painted with scenes of the king among the gods of the underworld. UNESCO lists the valley as part of *Ancient Thebes with its Necropolis*, inscribed in 1979.

## How tickets work {#tickets}

- A standard ticket covers a selection of tombs.
- Some of the most famous tombs, including Tutankhamun's and Seti I's, need a separate ticket.
- The tombs open to visitors rotate, so check which are open on the day.

> **Choosing your tombs**
> Ask your guide or the ticket office which tombs are open, and pick a mix: a long corridor tomb, a well-painted one and a smaller one. Your guide can explain the scenes before you go in.

## Tutankhamun {#tutankhamun}

Tutankhamun's tomb is small, but it is the only royal tomb in the valley that escaped looting in antiquity. Its treasures were brought out after 1922, and the full collection is now shown together at the [Grand Egyptian Museum](/guides/day-trips-from-cairo/) in Giza. Seeing the tomb in Luxor and the treasures in Giza tells the whole story.

## The rest of the West Bank {#west-bank}

The valley is usually combined with other West Bank sites:

- **Hatshepsut's temple** at Deir el-Bahari, set against the cliffs.
- **The Valley of the Queens,** with the tomb of Nefertari (separate ticket).
- **Medinet Habu,** the temple of Ramesses III, with richly carved reliefs.
- **Deir el-Medina,** the village of the craftsmen who built the royal tombs.
- **The Colossi of Memnon** by the roadside.

Our [Valley of the Kings and Hatshepsut Temple](/experiences/valley-of-the-kings-hatshepsut-temple/) day covers the classic circuit with a guide.

## Practical tips {#tips}

- Go early. The valley heats up fast and there is little shade.
- Bring water, a hat and small notes.
- Photography rules inside the tombs vary. Follow the guards' instructions.
- Plan the East Bank, Karnak and Luxor Temple, for a separate day. See our [Luxor guide](/destinations/luxor/).

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? How many tombs can I see?
A standard ticket covers a selection, and some famous tombs have their own tickets. Check on the day.

?? Is Tutankhamun's tomb worth the extra ticket?
For its history, yes. It is small and simple compared with the great corridor tombs, and its treasures are in Giza.

?? How long do I need for the West Bank?
A full day covers the valley, Hatshepsut's temple and one more site. With more time, add Medinet Habu and Deir el-Medina.
