## What it is {#what-it-is}

A full day from Cairo into the desert of the Fayoum, to Wadi Al-Hitan, the Whale Valley. Its fossils show the earliest whales in the last stages of losing their hind legs, and UNESCO inscribed it as a World Heritage site in 2005. The valley lies in the Wadi El-Rayan Protected Area, which also holds lakes and waterfalls.

## The day {#the-day}

- **Whale Valley.** Footpaths lead to the fossil whale skeletons, about 40 million years old, lying in the open sand. Vehicles stay out of the site itself.
- **The valley.** Around the fossils, rock formations and sand, with shark teeth and shells from the same ancient sea.
- **Wadi El-Rayan.** The lakes and waterfalls of Wadi El-Rayan, formed by drainage water from the farmland of the Fayoum.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? Why is it called Whale Valley?
Because of the fossil skeletons of early whales found there. *Wadi Al-Hitan* means "valley of the whales" in Arabic.

?? How old are the fossils?
About 40 million years old, according to Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency.

?? Can I visit Wadi Al-Hitan in a day from Cairo?
Yes. It is a full day, and the option on this page picks you up at your hotel in Cairo or Giza.

?? Is Wadi Al-Hitan a World Heritage site?
Yes. UNESCO inscribed it in 2005, as Egypt's natural World Heritage site.
