Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities calls the Temple of Kalabsha a beautiful and remarkably well-preserved monument, and the largest free-standing temple in Lower Nubia. It now stands on New Kalabsha Island near Aswan. This guide uses the facts on the Ministry's page.

## A Roman temple for a Nubian god {#history}

The temple was built under Augustus (30 BC–14 AD), the first Roman emperor. It was dedicated mainly to the Nubian god Mandulis, but Isis and her husband Osiris were worshipped here too.

Many cultures left their mark on it: a Nubian god, inscriptions in Meroitic and Greek, and slender, widely spaced columns that show the influence of Graeco-Roman tradition. Crosses carved on its walls show the arrival of Christianity in the region, when the sanctuary was used as a church.

## Inside the temple {#inside}

The temple follows a standard plan:

1. **The pylon,** a monumental gateway.
2. **An open forecourt.**
3. **A hypostyle hall,** a hall of columns.
4. **Two transverse chambers,** used as halls for offerings.
5. **The sanctuary,** at the far end, where the god's cult image was kept.

Outside the main temple are a partly rock-cut chapel to the Nubian god Dedwen, to the south-west, and a small chapel to the north-east, probably built by Ptolemy IX in the second century BC.

## The rescue {#rescue}

In 1962–1963, like many temples in Nubia, Kalabsha was moved from its original site on Kalabsha Island to New Kalabsha Island, to save it from the rising waters of the Nile after the building of the Aswan High Dam.

The Nubian monuments from Abu Simbel to Philae, including those on New Kalabsha, were inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in 1979.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? Who built the Temple of Kalabsha?
It was built under Augustus (30 BC–14 AD), the first Roman emperor, mainly for the Nubian god Mandulis.

?? Why was the Temple of Kalabsha moved?
To save it from the rising waters of the Nile after the Aswan High Dam was built. It was moved in 1962–1963 from Kalabsha Island to New Kalabsha Island.

?? Is the Temple of Kalabsha a World Heritage site?
Yes. It is one of the Nubian monuments from Abu Simbel to Philae, inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in 1979.

?? Why are there crosses in the Temple of Kalabsha?
They were carved when the sanctuary was used as a church, at the beginning of Christianity in the region.
