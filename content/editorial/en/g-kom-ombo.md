Kom Ombo is one of the most unusual temples in Egypt. It is dedicated to two gods at once, and it was built to match: two entrances, two axes and two sanctuaries, side by side under one roof. It stands north of Aswan, and most visitors see it on a Nile cruise. This guide explains what you are looking at.

## A temple for two gods {#two-gods}

The name comes from the Arabic *kum*, "mound", found in the names of many archaeological sites, and "Ombo", which goes back to the ancient Egyptian *Nubt*, understood as "the golden (city)".

The temple is dedicated to Sobek, the crocodile god, and to Har-wer, Horus the Elder, the falcon god. An earlier temple already stood here in the New Kingdom (about 1550–1069 BC), but the building you see dates from the Graeco-Roman period. The earliest royal name in it is that of Ptolemy VI Philometor (180–145 BC), and most of its decoration was completed under Ptolemy XII Neos Dionysos (80–51 BC).

## Two axes, two sanctuaries {#design}

Because it serves two gods, the temple has two parallel passages running through its columned halls, each ending in its own sanctuary.

- **The southern side (on the right)** belongs to Sobek, a god of fertility linked with water, the Nile flood and vegetation. He was worshipped here with his wife Hathor, goddess of love and motherhood, and their son Khonsu, the moon god.
- **The northern side (on the left)** belongs to Har-wer: Horus as an adult, the son of Osiris and Isis, victorious over Seth, the murderer of his father. He was worshipped with his wife Ta-senet-nefret, "the Perfect Companion", and their son Pa-neb-tawy, "the Lord of the Two Lands".

## What you'll see {#what-you-will-see}

- **The columns:** beautiful column capitals in the halls.
- **The calendars:** wall inscriptions that list festivals and other rites, with their dates and rituals.
- **The "surgical instruments":** a scene carved in the Roman period, in the 2nd century AD, showing what are believed to be surgical instruments, a sign of how advanced ancient Egyptian medicine was.
- **The back wall:** in the centre of the very back of the temple, Sobek and Har-wer appear, each on his own side, in a deeply symbolic scene.

## How to visit {#visiting}

- **On a Nile cruise:** cruises between Aswan and Luxor stop at Kom Ombo. See our [Nile cruise from Aswan to Luxor](/experiences/nile-cruise-aswan-to-luxor/).
- **By road:** you can also stop at Kom Ombo on a road transfer between Aswan and Luxor, with a driver or on a tour, and combine it with Edfu.
- **Tickets and opening hours:** these change, so check the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities' website before you go.

> **Reading the temple**
> Stand in the middle of the halls and look along each passage: the right side is Sobek's, the left side Horus'. A guide can point out the calendars and the instruments scene.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? Who is Kom Ombo temple dedicated to?
To two gods: Sobek, the crocodile god, and Har-wer, Horus the Elder, the falcon god.

?? Why does Kom Ombo have two of everything?
Because it serves two gods. Two parallel passages run through its halls to two sanctuaries: Sobek's on the right (south) and Horus' on the left (north).

?? How old is the temple?
The present building dates from the Graeco-Roman period. The earliest royal name in it is Ptolemy VI's (180–145 BC), and most of the decoration is from Ptolemy XII (80–51 BC). An earlier temple stood here in the New Kingdom.

?? Can I visit Kom Ombo without a cruise?
Yes, on a road transfer between Aswan and Luxor with a driver or a tour, often together with Edfu.
