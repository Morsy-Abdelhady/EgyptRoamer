The Red Sea is one of the reasons many people come to Egypt. Its reefs start close to shore, and a few sites are famous among divers worldwide. This guide covers three of them, what makes each special, and what to know before you book.

## The SS Thistlegorm {#thistlegorm}

The *Thistlegorm* was a British cargo steamship built in Sunderland in 1940. In 1941 she sailed for Egypt with supplies for the Allied forces, and she was waiting at anchor in the Red Sea when German bombers sank her on 6 October 1941.

Most of her cargo stayed inside the ship: trucks, motorcycles, Wellington boots, rifles and aircraft parts. Two steam locomotives carried on deck were thrown to either side of the wreck. Jacques Cousteau found the wreck in the early 1950s, and recreational diving on it began in the 1990s, as Sharm El Sheikh grew as a dive resort. It lies near Ras Mohammed National Park and is one of the best-known wreck dives in the world.

## Ras Mohammed {#ras-mohammed}

At the southern tip of the Sinai Peninsula, between the Gulf of Suez and the Gulf of Aqaba, Ras Mohammed was declared Egypt's first national park in 1983. Shark Reef and Yolanda Reef are its best-known dive sites, and it is also one of the great places to snorkel. ## The Blue Hole at Dahab {#blue-hole}

The Blue Hole is a submarine sinkhole on the Sinai coast just north of Dahab, on the Gulf of Aqaba. Its rim is rich in coral and reef fish, and divers and snorkellers enjoy its shallow parts.

> **The Arch is not a recreational dive**
> A deep tunnel called the Arch links the hole to the open sea. It has a reputation as one of the deadliest dive sites in the world, mostly because divers without the right training try to swim through it. Local operators require technical diving qualifications for the Arch. Recreational divers stay at the shallower sites nearby.

## Before you book a dive {#before-you-book}

- **Certification:** bring your certification card and logbook. If you are new to diving, choose a trip with a try-dive and an instructor.
- **Operators:** dive only with licensed operators and follow the guide's briefing.
- **Protect the reef:** never touch, stand on or collect coral, and use reef-friendly sun protection.
- **Flying after diving:** leave enough time between your last dive and your flight home. Ask your operator.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? Can beginners dive the Thistlegorm?
It is usually dived by certified divers on a guided boat trip. Ask the operator what level they require.

?? Is the Blue Hole dangerous?
The Arch is, for anyone without technical training. Recreational divers and snorkellers stay in the shallower parts, with a guide.

?? Hurghada or Sharm El Sheikh for diving?
Both are good bases. Sharm El Sheikh is closer to Ras Mohammed and the Thistlegorm; Hurghada has its own reefs and islands, such as Giftun.
