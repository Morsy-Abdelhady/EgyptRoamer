Nabq, in South Sinai, joins the sea and the desert in one protected area: coral reefs and dense mangroves on the shore, and mountains and valleys inland. This guide is based on the facts given by Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency, which manages the country's protected areas.

## The protected area in brief {#in-brief}

- **Declared:** 1992.
- **Area:** 518 km².
- **Type:** protected area for the sustainable use of natural resources.
- **Where:** South Sinai Governorate, about 500 km from Cairo.

## Sea and desert {#ecosystems}

The Agency lists several important ecosystems in Nabq:

- **Coral reefs** and the creatures of the sea.
- **Mangrove forests,** which grow here very densely.
- **Desert and mountain systems,** with valleys.

## Wildlife and people {#wildlife}

Nabq is home to gazelles, ibex, hyenas and reptiles, many migratory and resident birds, and invertebrates. Some Bedouin tribes live in the area.

## How to visit {#visiting}

- **What to do:** the Agency describes Nabq as a draw for divers, desert safaris and birdwatchers.
- **Protect the place:** never touch or take coral, shells or plants, and leave no rubbish.
- **Entry fees and rules:** they can change, so check with the Environmental Affairs Agency or your operator before you go.
