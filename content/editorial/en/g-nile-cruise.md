Between Luxor and Aswan, the Nile passes some of the best-preserved temples in Egypt. You can see the stretch in two ways: on a cruise, where the boat is your hotel and moves between the sites, or by staying in hotels in each city and travelling between them by road or train. Both work well. This guide explains the difference so you can choose.

## What a Nile cruise is like {#cruise}

A Luxor–Aswan cruise runs in either direction. Your cabin, meals and the visits are usually arranged together, and a guide takes the group to the temples at each stop. Between visits there is time on deck to watch the river: palm groves, villages and desert on either side.

> **Why people love it**
> Unpacking once, and seeing the river itself. For many travellers the slow days on deck are the best memory of Egypt.

## The temples on the way {#temples}

### Edfu
The Temple of Horus at Edfu is one of the best-preserved temples in Egypt. It was begun under Ptolemy III in 237 BC and completed in 57 BC, and its simple plan along one main axis is the classic example of an Egyptian temple.

### Kom Ombo
A double temple of the Ptolemaic and Roman periods on a bend of the river, dedicated to two gods at once: Sobek, the crocodile god, and Horus, the falcon-headed god.

Cruises stop at both. By road, they can be visited on the transfer day between Luxor and [Aswan](/destinations/aswan/).

## Cruise or hotels: the trade-offs {#trade-offs}

- **Time at the sites.** Cruise visits follow the boat's schedule and are often short. Staying in hotels lets you spend longer at Karnak, the West Bank and Philae.
- **Freedom.** In hotels you choose where to eat and when to rest. On a boat the day has a set rhythm.
- **Effort.** A cruise means unpacking once and no transfers. The hotel route needs one road or train day between the cities.
- **The river.** Only a cruise gives you days on the water.

Many people combine the two: a cruise, plus an extra day in [Luxor](/destinations/luxor/) before or after for the sites the boat schedule rushes.

## Smaller boats {#smaller-boats}

Besides the large cruise ships, smaller boats sail the same stretch. Traditional sailing boats called dahabiyas carry far fewer passengers, and some moor at quieter places along the bank. In Aswan, a felucca, the local lateen-sailed boat, is the way to see the river for an afternoon.

## How to choose {#how-to-choose}

- **Choose a cruise** if you want an easy, relaxed trip with the river as the highlight.
- **Choose hotels** if the monuments are your priority and you like to set your own pace.
- **Either way,** plan the [Valley of the Kings](/experiences/valley-of-the-kings-hatshepsut-temple/) in Luxor and [Abu Simbel](/experiences/abu-simbel-day-trip-from-aswan/) from Aswan, which are outside the usual cruise route.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? Is it better to start in Luxor or in Aswan?
Both work. Starting in Aswan ends the trip in Luxor, which has more to see afterwards. Starting in Luxor ends it in Aswan's calmer atmosphere.

?? Does a cruise include Abu Simbel?
Usually not. Abu Simbel lies far south of Aswan and is visited as a separate trip from the city, or on a Lake Nasser cruise.

?? Are the temple visits guided?
On most cruises, yes. Check what is included when you book.
