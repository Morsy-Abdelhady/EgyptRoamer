The Temple of Edfu is one of the most striking and complete of all ancient Egyptian temples. It stands on the west bank of the Nile between Aswan and Luxor, and it is dedicated to Horus, the falcon god. Most visitors see it on a Nile cruise. This guide explains what you are looking at.

## A temple built over 180 years {#history}

Building began under Ptolemy III, in 237 BC, and the temple was completed under Ptolemy XII in 57 BC, 180 years later. Its plan, laid out along one main axis, is the classic example of an Egyptian temple.

For centuries the temple lay buried under the debris of the town that grew up around it. That is why its walls and carvings have survived so well. In 1860 the French archaeologist Auguste Mariette uncovered it and restored parts of it.

## What you'll see {#what-you-will-see}

You walk through the temple in the order its priests did, from the great gateway to the sanctuary.

- **The pylon:** two massive towers carved with scenes of Ptolemy XII defeating his enemies and worshipping the gods.
- **The falcons of Horus:** two large granite statues of Horus as a falcon stand before the pylon.
- **The court:** a large open court lined with columns with floral capitals.
- **Two hypostyle halls:** the first shows the founding of the temple, with the king at worship; the second shows Horus travelling in his sacred boat with the goddess Hathor.
- **The sanctuary:** beyond a transverse hall, at the heart of the temple.

## How to visit {#visiting}

- **On a Nile cruise:** cruises between Aswan and Luxor stop at Edfu on the way. See our [Nile cruise from Aswan to Luxor](/experiences/nile-cruise-aswan-to-luxor/).
- **By road:** you can also stop at Edfu on a road transfer between Aswan and Luxor, with a driver or on a tour.
- **Tickets and opening hours:** these change, so check the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities' website before you go.

> **With a guide**
> The scenes on the walls tell the story of Horus and of the temple itself. An Egyptologist guide brings them to life.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? Who is the Temple of Edfu dedicated to?
To Horus, the falcon god.

?? How old is the Temple of Edfu?
It was built between 237 and 57 BC, under the Ptolemies.

?? Why is the Temple of Edfu so well preserved?
It lay buried under the debris of the later town for centuries, which protected its walls and carvings.

?? Can I visit Edfu without a cruise?
Yes, on a road transfer between Aswan and Luxor with a driver or a tour.
