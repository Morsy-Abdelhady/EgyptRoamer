The Nubia Museum in Aswan tells the story of Nubia, the land along the Nile south of Aswan. Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities says its thousands of objects show how Egyptian and Nubian civilisation developed, in geography, society and culture. This guide uses the facts on the Ministry's page.

## Why the museum was built {#history}

The museum grew out of the international campaign to save the monuments of ancient Nubia, which UNESCO launched in 1960 at the request of the Egyptian government. Work on the museum began in the early 1980s, when a committee was formed of specialists from the Supreme Council of Antiquities, Egyptian universities and UNESCO.

## The building {#building}

The building was designed by the architect Dr. Mahmoud Al-Hakim. The interior was designed by the Mexican architect Pedro Ramírez Vázquez, who was asked to show the archaeological, historical, cultural and natural heritage of Nubia.

The museum opened in 1997. Its walls are clad in local sandstone and pink granite, in a style that reflects traditional Nubian architecture. In 2001 it won the Aga Khan Award for Architecture.

## Inside the museum {#inside}

- **Three floors** of galleries, with thousands of objects.
- **The main hall and the diorama** show the traditions and handicrafts of Nubia.
- The museum also has a lecture hall, a library, an education department, a theatre and an open-air amphitheatre for Nubian folklore.

## The garden {#garden}

The garden works as an open-air museum. In it you will find:

- part of a Fatimid cemetery;
- a cave with prehistoric rock inscriptions;
- a waterway that stands for the Nile, with channels that show how the river and the Nubian village lived together, among plants that grew in ancient times;
- a reconstructed Nubian house, a glimpse of daily life in Nubia.

## Rules for visitors {#rules}

The Ministry's page lists the museum's rules. The main ones:

- **Photos:** private photography is allowed after you pay the ticket fee. No flash, and don't photograph other visitors or staff. A camera stand needs its own ticket.
- **Bags:** large bags, backpacks and parcels bigger than 40 × 40 cm, umbrellas and sharp objects go in the cloakroom.
- **Food and drink:** not allowed in the galleries, except small bottles of water. No smoking.
- **Children** must stay with an adult at all times. No pets.

## How to visit {#visiting}

- **Evening opening:** the Ministry lists evening hours on Thursdays and Fridays, as well as the daytime opening.
- **Last tickets:** the ticket window closes an hour before the museum.
- **Tickets and opening hours:** these change, and Ramadan has its own hours, so check the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities' website before you go.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

?? When did the Nubia Museum open?
In 1997. Work on it began in the early 1980s, following UNESCO's campaign to save the monuments of Nubia.

?? What is there to see at the Nubia Museum?
Three floors of finds on the history of Nubia, a diorama of Nubian traditions and crafts, and a garden with a Fatimid cemetery, a cave with prehistoric rock inscriptions and a reconstructed Nubian house.

?? Who designed the Nubia Museum?
The architect Dr. Mahmoud Al-Hakim designed the building, and the Mexican architect Pedro Ramírez Vázquez designed the interior. It won the Aga Khan Award for Architecture in 2001.

?? Can I take photos in the Nubia Museum?
Yes, private photography is allowed after you pay the ticket fee. Flash is not allowed.
