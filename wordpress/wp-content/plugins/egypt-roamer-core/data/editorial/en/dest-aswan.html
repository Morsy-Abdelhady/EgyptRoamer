Aswan is Egypt at its most relaxed. Here the Nile narrows between granite boulders and green islands, and the desert comes right down to the water on the west bank. It is also the heart of Nubian culture. Many travellers arrive exhausted from temple-hopping and find that Aswan is where the trip slows down: a felucca at sunset, dinner on a terrace over the river, and one early start for Abu Simbel.

Why go

  • The most beautiful stretch of the Nile. Islands, rocks, sails and dunes, best seen from the water.
  • Nubian culture. Colourful villages, music and food distinct from the rest of Egypt.
  • The gateway to Abu Simbel and the Nubian temples. UNESCO lists them as Nubian Monuments from Abu Simbel to Philae.
  • Where Nile cruises start and end. The Luxor–Aswan cruise and the Lake Nasser cruises both begin here.

On the water

A felucca, the traditional lateen-sailed boat, is the way to see Aswan. Sail around Elephantine Island and Kitchener's Island, home to the Aswan Botanical Garden, and past the rocks of the first cataract as the light goes golden. Boats leave from the Corniche. Agree the duration and the price before you board.

Philae and the monuments

Philae Temple

The temple of Isis stands on its island in the reservoir between the dams. It was dismantled and rebuilt on higher ground on Agilkia Island when the dams raised the water, and it's reached by a short boat ride. It's one of the most graceful temples in Egypt.

The Unfinished Obelisk

A giant obelisk abandoned in the granite quarry where it was being cut. It's a rare look at how the ancient Egyptians worked stone.

Elephantine Island

Ruins of ancient Abu, one of the oldest settlements in the region, with a Nilometer and Nubian villages among the palms.

Tombs of the Nobles and the west bank

Rock tombs on the hill of Qubbet el-Hawa, with wide views over the river. Nearby are the desert ruins of the Monastery of St Simeon.

The Nubian Museum

A well-designed museum on Nubian history and culture, including the story of the villages lost beneath Lake Nasser.

The High Dam

The dam that created Lake Nasser changed Egypt, and flooded Nubia. The views and the history matter more than the structure itself.

Nubian villages

The Nubian villages on the west bank, such as Gharb Soheil, are known for their painted houses, domed roofs and warm hospitality. Visits are usually by boat. The atmosphere varies from genuinely local to quite commercial. Go with an open mind, buy crafts directly from the makers, and ask before photographing people.

Abu Simbel

The two rock temples of Ramesses II and Nefertari at Abu Simbel lie far to the south, near the Sudanese border. In the 1960s they were cut into blocks and moved to higher ground to save them from Lake Nasser. Most visitors come on a day trip from Aswan, leaving before dawn by road or taking a short flight.

The Abu Simbel sun festival

Twice a year, on or around 22 February and 22 October, the rising sun reaches deep into the Great Temple's sanctuary and lights the seated statues at its back. The event draws large crowds. Plan and book early if you want to be there.

Between Aswan and Luxor

On the way north, the temples of Kom Ombo, a double temple on a bend of the river, and Edfu, one of the best-preserved temples in Egypt, are the classic stops. Nile cruises visit both. By road, they can be seen on a transfer day to Luxor.

How long, and when to go

How long. Two to three days: one for Philae, the Unfinished Obelisk and a felucca, one for Abu Simbel, and a slower day for the islands and a Nubian village.

When to go. October to April is the best time. Summer is extremely hot, often the hottest in Egypt, so move early and rest at midday.

Getting there

  • Aswan has an airport with flights from Cairo.
  • Trains, including sleeper trains, run from Cairo and Luxor.
  • It's the southern end of Luxor–Aswan cruises.

In town, the Corniche and the ferries make getting around easy. Use taxis or a driver for Philae and the High Dam.

Frequently asked questions

Is Abu Simbel worth the long trip?

For most people, yes. The temples are among the most extraordinary in Egypt, and their rescue from Lake Nasser is part of the story. If the early road trip is too much, consider flying.

How many days should I spend in Aswan?

Two full days plus the Abu Simbel trip is ideal. Aswan is also a good place to rest, so an extra day is rarely wasted.

Should I start my Nile cruise in Aswan or Luxor?

Both work. Starting in Aswan ends the cruise in Luxor, which has more to see afterwards. Starting in Luxor ends the trip in Aswan's calmer atmosphere.

Is it respectful to visit Nubian villages?

Yes, if done thoughtfully. Go with a local operator, buy directly from artisans, and ask before taking photos of people or homes.
