At dawn, balloons rise over the West Bank of Luxor, above the fields, the temples and the cliffs of the Theban hills. For many visitors it is the most memorable hour of the trip. This guide explains how the flights work, what can stop them, and how to plan one.

What the flight is

Balloons take off from a launch site on Luxor's West Bank and fly in the early morning, from sunrise into the first hours after it. From the basket you look over the farmland along the Nile and the archaeological sites of the West Bank, with the river and the East Bank behind them.

Several companies operate flights. According to Egypt Today, their baskets range from 2 to 32 passengers, in eight models. A smaller basket means fewer people beside you, and the option you book states which kind it is.

Who decides if you fly

Flights take place under the supervision of the Egyptian Civil Aviation Authority. Each morning depends on the weather: on 13 March 2026, for example, the Authority cancelled the West Bank flights after warnings of strong winds and unsettled weather in the early morning.

Keep a spare morning

A flight can be cancelled at short notice for wind or poor visibility. Book it early in your stay in Luxor, so a cancelled morning can still be moved.

The summer pause

In 2026, for the first time in 38 years of ballooning in Luxor, commercial flights were suspended for the summer, from 1 June to 30 August. The Civil Aviation Authority and the Egyptian Airports Company announced it at the request of all the operating companies, citing the high summer temperatures; Egypt Today describes it as a pause to be applied every year. Flights resumed in September. If you travel in summer, check that flights are running before you plan around one.

Safety

Ballooning carries real risks. On 26 February 2013, a balloon caught fire while landing near Luxor and 19 of the 21 people on board died; the Civil Aviation Authority's report blamed a leak in an ageing fuel line. After an earlier crash, in 2009, flights had been grounded for six months, pilot training was increased and the balloons were given a designated launch site.

  • Follow the crew: listen to the briefing, especially the landing position, and keep to it.
  • Read the option: check on the partner's page what the flight includes before you book.
  • Weather: a cancellation is a safety decision. Don't look for a company that will fly when others don't.

Planning your flight

  • An early start: pick-ups are well before sunrise, because the flight is timed to the dawn.
  • With a cruise: some Nile cruises from Aswan to Luxor include a balloon flight, others offer it as an extra. See what each option includes on our Nile cruise page.
  • The rest of the day: after landing you are already on the West Bank. Combine the flight with the tombs and temples: read our Valley of the Kings guide and our West Bank tour.

Frequently asked questions

Where do the balloons fly?

Over Luxor's West Bank: the fields along the Nile and the archaeological sites, with the river and the East Bank in view.

Can a flight be cancelled?

Yes. The Civil Aviation Authority can cancel flights for wind or unstable weather, as it did on 13 March 2026. Keep a spare morning.

Do balloons fly in summer?

In 2026, commercial flights paused from 1 June to 30 August at the operators' request, because of the summer heat. Check before you travel in those months.

Is a balloon flight safe?

Flights are supervised by the Egyptian Civil Aviation Authority, but there have been fatal accidents, the worst in 2013. Follow the crew's instructions and accept weather cancellations.
