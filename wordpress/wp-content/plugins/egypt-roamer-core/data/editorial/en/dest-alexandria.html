Alexandria feels like a different country from Cairo. It was founded by Alexander the Great and became the Ptolemies' capital, and for centuries it was the Greek-speaking, cosmopolitan face of Egypt. The ancient city lies mostly beneath the modern one, or under the sea, so Alexandria rewards curiosity more than checklists. Come for the Corniche, the sea light, the old cafés and the fish, and let the monuments fill the gaps.

Why go

  • A Mediterranean city with its own rhythm. A long seafront Corniche, sea breezes and a café culture closer to the Levant than to the Nile Valley.
  • Layers of history. Pharaonic, Greek, Roman, Christian, Islamic and 19th-century European Alexandria, often in the same street.
  • An easy change of pace. It's a natural two- or three-day break from Cairo's intensity, or a first stop before heading south.

Layers of history: what to see

The Citadel of Qaitbay

The Mamluk sultan Qaitbay built this sea fort in the 15th century on the site of the ancient Pharos lighthouse, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, and reused its stone. Walk the ramparts for the view back along the Eastern Harbour.

The Bibliotheca Alexandrina

The modern library is a tribute to the ancient Library of Alexandria. Its tilted disc of a building faces the sea, and inside are museums and exhibitions as well as the vast reading room.

Kom el-Shoqafa catacombs

A Roman-era burial complex cut deep into the rock. Its decoration mixes Egyptian gods with Greco-Roman style, a perfect snapshot of Alexandria's fusion culture.

Pompey's Pillar and the Serapeum

A single towering granite column on the hill of the ancient Serapeum temple. It was named after Pompey by mistake: it was raised for the emperor Diocletian.

Kom el-Dikka Roman theatre

A small, well-preserved Roman theatre with the remains of baths and lecture halls around it, uncovered in the middle of the modern city.

Alexandria National Museum

A compact museum in a restored mansion. It takes you through the city's history, from pharaonic objects to finds from the harbour.

Montaza Palace gardens

The former royal palace grounds at the eastern end of the Corniche, with gardens and small beaches. It's popular with local families.

The Corniche and city life

The Corniche curves along the Eastern Harbour and beyond. It's the city's promenade, its traffic artery and its meeting place, especially in the evening. Downtown, around Saad Zaghloul Square and the streets behind it, old patisseries and cafés are a reminder of Alexandria's cosmopolitan era. Take time to sit in one.

Food: the sea on the table

Alexandria is Egypt's seafood city. The classic way to eat is to choose your fish and seafood from the ice at a fish restaurant. It's weighed, and grilled or fried as you ask. Look for:

  • Sayadeya: fish cooked with caramelised onions and spiced rice.
  • Grilled fish and prawns, with tahini, salads and bread.
  • Liver sandwiches (kebda iskandarani), Alexandria-style: spicy, with peppers.
  • Pastries and ice cream from the long-established patisseries downtown.

Ordering fish

Prices at fish restaurants are usually by weight. Confirm the price and how it will be cooked before it goes to the kitchen.

Day trips

  • Rosetta (Rashid). The town where the Rosetta Stone was found, with Ottoman-era merchant houses, on the Nile's western mouth.
  • El Alamein. The Second World War battlefield, with its war cemeteries and military museum, west along the coast.

How long, and when to go

How long. Two full days cover the main sites and leave time for the Corniche and the cafés. A day trip from Cairo is possible, but the round trip eats into it. Staying at least one night is far better.

When to go.

  • Spring and autumn are the most pleasant seasons.
  • Summer is the Egyptian holiday season on the coast: busy, humid and lively.
  • Winter is cooler, with rain and wind off the sea on some days.

Getting there and around

  • Trains run between Cairo and Alexandria throughout the day, and they're the most comfortable way to arrive.
  • Road transfers and buses also connect the two cities.
  • Borg El Arab airport serves Alexandria by air.
  • In town: ride-hailing apps and taxis are easy. The old yellow trams are slow, but they're part of the experience.

Frequently asked questions

Is Alexandria worth visiting on a short trip to Egypt?

If you have a week or less, the Nile Valley usually comes first. With more time, Alexandria adds a completely different side of Egypt: Mediterranean, Greco-Roman and cosmopolitan.

Can I see the ancient Lighthouse or the Library?

Neither survives. The Citadel of Qaitbay stands on the lighthouse's site, and the modern Bibliotheca Alexandrina honours the ancient library.

Can I swim in Alexandria?

The city beaches are busy and mostly used by local families in summer. For a beach holiday, the resorts further west along the coast or the Red Sea are better choices.

Where do I go after Alexandria?

Back through Cairo to the Nile Valley — Luxor and Aswan — or to the Red Sea at Hurghada.
