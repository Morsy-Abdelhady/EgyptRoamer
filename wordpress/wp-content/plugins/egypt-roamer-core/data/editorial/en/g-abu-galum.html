At Abu Galum, in South Sinai, the mountains come right down to the beaches. Coral reefs lie offshore and wild valleys run inland. This guide is based on the facts given by Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency, which manages the country's protected areas.

The protected area in brief

  • Declared: 1992.
  • Area: 425.7 km².
  • Type: protected area for the sustainable use of natural resources.
  • Where: South Sinai Governorate, about 500 km from Cairo.

Mountains by the sea

What makes Abu Galum special, according to the Agency, is its landscape: the mountains stand close to the shore. The protected area holds many ecosystems:

  • Coral reefs and the creatures of the sea.
  • Seagrass and lagoons.
  • Desert and mountain systems.

Plants and wildlife

The mountains and valleys are rich in animals, birds and wild plants. The protected area has about 165 plant species, 44 of which are found only in this area.

The underwater caves

Abu Galum is known for an underwater cave system that reaches depths of more than 100 m. The Agency warns that this system is unstable and extremely dangerous, and that it must be protected, along with the ecosystem of the protected area.

How to visit

  • What to do: the Agency describes Abu Galum as a draw for divers, desert safaris and watchers of birds and animals.
  • The caves are not for recreational diving: the Agency calls them unstable and extremely dangerous.
  • Protect the place: never touch or take coral, shells or plants, and leave no rubbish.
  • Entry fees and rules: they can change, so check with the Environmental Affairs Agency or your operator before you go.
