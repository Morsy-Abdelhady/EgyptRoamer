The Mummification Museum in Luxor, ancient Thebes, is devoted to one question: how did the ancient Egyptians preserve the body? Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities describes it as a museum that gives a comprehensive view of the whole process. It pairs well with a day among the tombs of the West Bank. This guide explains what you will see.

Preserving the body

According to the Ministry, mummification is believed to have taken around 70 days, accompanied by many rituals:

  1. The organs were carefully removed through a small incision, about 10 cm long, in the left side of the body, and preserved in canopic jars.
  2. The body was dried in natron, the salt brought from Wadi El Natron, for about 40 days.
  3. It was wrapped in linen bandages, with magical amulets placed within the wrappings on different parts of the body to protect the dead.
  4. The family then received the body and placed it in a coffin for burial.

The god of embalming and mummification was Anubis, the jackal god.

Not only people

The ancient Egyptians embalmed not only people but many animals too, such as cats, dogs and crocodiles. Among the highlights the Ministry lists are a mummified crocodile and even a mummified fish, a Nile perch.

What you'll see

The museum displays the tools, objects and equipment used in mummification, and explains the ritual and religious meaning of the practice. On display are canopic jars, richly decorated coffins, mummified remains, amulets and statues of gods.

Highlights listed by the Ministry include:

  • The mummy and coffin of Maseharti, with details of the coffin's decoration.
  • Statues of Anubis and of Nephthys.
  • An ankh amulet, the sign of life.
  • Ushabti figurines and a painted wooden chest of ushabtis.
  • Unguent jars.

Before you visit

  • Photography: private photography is allowed after paying for your ticket, without flash and without tripods.
  • Bags: large bags and parcels over 40 × 40 cm go to the cloakroom.
  • Tickets and opening hours: these change, so check the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities' website before you go.

Frequently asked questions

How long did mummification take?

According to Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities, the process is believed to have taken around 70 days, including about 40 days of drying the body in natron.

What are canopic jars?

The jars in which the organs removed from the body were preserved. Several are on display in the museum.

Did the ancient Egyptians mummify animals?

Yes. They embalmed many animals, such as cats, dogs and crocodiles. The museum shows a mummified crocodile and a mummified Nile perch.

Can I take photos in the Mummification Museum?

The Ministry says private photography is allowed after paying the ticket, without flash and without tripods.
