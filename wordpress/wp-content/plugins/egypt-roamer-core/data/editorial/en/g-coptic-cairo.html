Inside the walls of an old Roman fortress in the south of Cairo stands one of the city's quietest and oldest quarters. Churches, a synagogue and the Coptic Museum sit side by side in a few lanes, and the Mosque of Amr ibn al-As is close by. This guide covers what to see in Coptic Cairo, also called Old Cairo.

The Roman fortress of Babylon

The quarter grew around and within the Roman fortress of Babylon, part of the area of al-Fustat. Its remains are still visible, and several buildings stand on top of them.

The Hanging Church

The church takes its name from being built over the ruins of the Roman fortress, so that it seems to hang above them. Inside, three rows of marble columns divide it into four aisles, and its wooden ceiling was made to recall Noah's Ark. Look for the marble pulpit on ten columns, which dates from the 11th century, and the icons on its walls. The oldest is from the 15th century, and most of the others from the late 18th century.

The church of Abu Serga

The church of Saints Sergius and Bacchus, known as Abu Serga, is named after two early 4th-century martyrs. Below its sanctuary lies a crypt. By tradition, this is the cave where the Holy Family sheltered during its journey through Egypt.

The Ben Ezra Synagogue

The oldest synagogue in Egypt, named after the scholar and philosopher Abraham ibn Ezra. It is no longer used for worship. Its hekhal, which marks the direction of prayer, is decorated in Arabesque style and inlaid with mother-of-pearl. Like every synagogue, it kept a geniza, a storeroom for old documents that could not be thrown away. Ben Ezra's held documents gathered over about 850 years. Found by Europeans in 1888, the Cairo Geniza is now kept in libraries around the world.

The Coptic Museum

The largest museum of Coptic history, antiquities and art, with about 16,000 objects. Marcus Simaika Pasha began building it in 1908 on land of the Coptic Church, and it opened in 1910. It became state property in 1931.

Visiting Coptic Cairo

  • These are places of worship. Dress modestly and keep quiet during services.
  • Mar Girgis metro station is beside the quarter, the easiest way to arrive.
  • Photography rules differ from building to building. Ask first.

Frequently asked questions

Is Coptic Cairo the same as Old Cairo?

Mostly. The name covers the churches, synagogue and museum within and around the Roman fortress, part of the old area of al-Fustat.

How much time do I need?

Half a day is enough for the main churches, the synagogue and the museum, at an easy pace.

Can I visit during services?

The churches are places of worship. If a service is on, keep quiet, stay at the back and do not take photos. Check opening on the day.
