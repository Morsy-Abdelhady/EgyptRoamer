Philae is the classic visit in Aswan: a temple of the goddess Isis on an island, reached by a short boat ride. It was one of the last places where the old gods of Egypt were worshipped, and it was saved from the waters of the High Dam by moving it, stone by stone, to a neighbouring island. This guide explains what you will see.

The temple of Isis

Most of the monuments of Philae date from the Ptolemaic period (332–30 BC). The most important is the temple of Isis, the mother of Horus, god of kingship. It was begun by Ptolemy II Philadelphus (285–246 BC).

In the mammisi, the birth house where the birth of Horus was celebrated, a scene shows Isis nursing her son Horus in the marshes.

The last temple of the old gods

The temple of Isis was one of the last ancient Egyptian temples to remain active. It kept working until the reign of the Byzantine emperor Justinian I (527–565 AD), who ordered all pagan temples closed. UNESCO describes Philae as the final bastion of ancient Egyptian religion.

It was here that a priest of Isis named Esmet-Akhom carved the very last dated hieroglyphic inscription, in 394 AD. The temple was later turned into a church, and many inscriptions were deliberately destroyed.

What else you'll see

  • The temple of Hathor: built by Ptolemy VI Philometor (180–145 BC) and Augustus, the first Roman emperor (30 BC–14 AD).
  • Trajan's Kiosk: built under the emperor Trajan (98–117 AD), it once stood at the front of the temple complex. Its roof has gone, but its rows of columns still stand, and travellers have described and drawn it for centuries. Its reliefs show the emperor making offerings to Isis, her consort Osiris and their son Horus.

Moved to Agilkia

All these monuments once stood on the island of Philae itself. When the Aswan High Dam threatened to flood them, they were moved to the nearby island of Agilkia, as part of the UNESCO campaign to save the monuments of Nubia, launched in the 1960s. Philae belongs to the World Heritage site Nubian Monuments from Abu Simbel to Philae, inscribed in 1979.

How to visit

  • By boat: the temple stands on an island, so the visit starts with a short boat ride.
  • In a day in Aswan: Philae fits well with the Unfinished Obelisk and a felucca on the Nile at sunset.
  • Tickets and opening hours: these change, so check the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities' website before you go.

Frequently asked questions

Who is the temple of Philae dedicated to?

To Isis, the mother of Horus. The island also has a temple of Hathor and the Kiosk of Trajan.

Is Philae in its original place?

No. The monuments were moved from the island of Philae to the nearby island of Agilkia, to save them from the waters of the Aswan High Dam, during the UNESCO campaign to save the monuments of Nubia.

Why is Philae important?

It was one of the last ancient Egyptian temples to stay active, until the reign of Justinian I, and the last dated hieroglyphic inscription was carved here in 394 AD.

How do I get to Philae?

By a short boat ride, since the temple stands on an island near Aswan.
