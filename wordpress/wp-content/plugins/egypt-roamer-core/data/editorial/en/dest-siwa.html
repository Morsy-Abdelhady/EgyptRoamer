Siwa is Egypt's most remote oasis and one of its most distinctive places. It lies deep in the Western Desert, close to the Libyan border, surrounded by salt lakes, date palms and the dunes of the Great Sand Sea. The people are Amazigh (Berber) and speak their own language, Siwi, as well as Arabic. Getting here is a commitment, and that's much of the appeal: Siwa is slow, quiet and unlike anywhere else in the country.

Is Siwa for you?

Siwa suits travellers who want landscape, silence and a traditional community rather than big monuments. It isn't a quick add-on. The road journey from Cairo or the coast takes most of a day in each direction. If you have a week or less in Egypt, the Nile Valley is the better choice. With ten days or more, and a taste for the desert, Siwa can be the highlight.

What to see

The Shali fortress

The ruined old town at the centre of Siwa is built of kershef, a mixture of salt, mud and rock. It has been slowly melting since heavy rains in the 20th century. Climb to the top at sunset for the view over the palm groves and lakes.

The Oracle of Amun

The temple of the oracle at Aghurmi, on a rock outcrop just outside town, was famous throughout the ancient world. In 331 BC Alexander the Great crossed the desert to consult it, and the oracle is said to have confirmed his divine status. The ruins are modest, but the history and the view are not.

Mountain of the Dead

Gebel al-Mawta is a hill honeycombed with ancient tombs. A few are painted, and it's worth a short visit.

Springs and salt lakes

  • Cleopatra's Spring: a deep natural pool used for swimming.
  • The salt lakes: the water is so saline that you float effortlessly. The bright white salt flats make striking landscapes.
  • Fatnas (Fantasy) Island: palm groves on the lake shore, and Siwa's classic sunset spot.

The Great Sand Sea

South of the oasis, the dunes of the Great Sand Sea begin. A 4×4 excursion into the dunes is essential: sand-boarding, hot and cold springs in the desert, and sunset over the dunes. Excursions are run by licensed local operators, who also handle any permits the desert areas require. See our Great Sand Sea 4×4 and hot springs trip.

Local life and customs

Siwa is a conservative, close-knit community.

Travelling respectfully in Siwa

  • Dress modestly: shoulders and knees covered for everyone, and loose clothing is best.
  • Ask before photographing people, and don't photograph women.
  • Alcohol is not part of local life. Don't expect it, and don't bring it into public places.
  • Buy local products directly: dates, olives, olive oil and Siwan embroidery.

How long, and when to go

How long. Plan at least three nights in the oasis, not counting travel days: one day for the town, the fortress and the oracle, one for the springs and lakes, and one for the Great Sand Sea.

When to go.

  • October to April is the season. Days are warm and nights can be surprisingly cold in winter, so bring layers.
  • Summer is very hot and best avoided.

Getting there and around

  • By road. Siwa is reached by road, usually via Marsa Matruh on the Mediterranean coast. Most visitors come by a pre-arranged transfer or on overnight or day buses from Cairo or Alexandria.
  • Around the oasis. Bicycles, donkey carts and tuk-tuks are the normal ways to get around the oasis.
  • The desert. Excursions require a 4×4 with a local driver.

Frequently asked questions

Is Siwa worth the journey?

If you love the desert and want somewhere completely different, absolutely. If your time is short or you prefer monuments, the Nile Valley is a better use of your days.

How many days do I need in Siwa?

Three nights in the oasis is a good minimum, plus a travel day each way.

Can I visit the desert on my own?

No. Desert trips need a 4×4 and a licensed local driver who knows the terrain and handles permits.

What should I combine Siwa with?

Alexandria and the Mediterranean coast are on the natural route. Some travellers continue to the Western Desert oases on a longer desert journey.
