El Omayed, in Matrouh Governorate, protects a landscape of sand dunes and inland plateaus that is rich in wild plants. This guide is based on the facts given by Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency, which manages the country's protected areas.

The protected area in brief

  • Declared: 1986.
  • Area: 87 km².
  • Type: protected area for the sustainable use of natural resources.
  • Where: Matrouh Governorate, about 300 km from Cairo.

A garden of wild plants

About 170 kinds of wild plants grow in the protected area's different environments, on the sand dunes and on the inland plateaus. Studies have shown that many of them are useful:

  • About 70 kinds are medicinal, among them squill and wormwood.
  • About 60 kinds have other uses: as fuel, as a source of oils and soap, as food, for gardens, for making ropes and roofs, and for grazing.
  • About 40 kinds matter for the environment, for example because they hold the sand in place.

Wildlife

The protected area is home to many wild animals, among them gazelles, foxes, hares, jerboas, hedgehogs, chameleons and scorpions. There are also 14 kinds of birds of prey.

How to visit

  • Protect the place: never pick or take plants, and leave no rubbish.
  • Entry fees and rules: they can change, so check with the Environmental Affairs Agency before you go.

Frequently asked questions

Where is El Omayed?

In Matrouh Governorate, about 300 km from Cairo.

What is El Omayed known for?

Its wild plants: about 170 kinds, of which about 70 are medicinal.

What animals live in El Omayed?

Among others, gazelles, foxes, hares, jerboas, hedgehogs, chameleons and scorpions, and 14 kinds of birds of prey.

When was El Omayed declared a protected area?

In 1986.
