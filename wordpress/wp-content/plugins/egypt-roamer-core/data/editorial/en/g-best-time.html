There is no single best month for Egypt, because the country covers very different climates. The Nile Valley has hot deserts on both sides. The Red Sea coasts have long, warm seasons. The Mediterranean coast is milder and wetter, and the Western Desert is extreme in both directions. The right time depends on what you plan to do. This guide goes region by region, then through the calendar.

The short answer

  • For temples and sightseeing (Cairo, Luxor, Aswan): October to April, when days are warm rather than scorching.
  • For the Red Sea (Hurghada, Sharm El Sheikh): most of the year. Spring and autumn give warm water without the summer heat.
  • For the desert (Siwa, the White Desert): October to April, but pack for cold nights.
  • For Alexandria and the north coast: spring and autumn. Summer is Egypt's own beach season.

Region by region

Cairo

Mild in winter, hot in summer. Sightseeing at Giza is the main thing affected by the heat, because the plateau is exposed. In the warm months, plan it for the early morning.

Luxor and Aswan

The heat increases the further south you go. From late spring to early autumn, Luxor and Aswan are among the hottest places in Egypt. Winter is the peak season, with the most comfortable conditions and the most visitors.

The Red Sea coast

Warm for most of the year. Winter is milder on land, with cooler water and more wind (good news for kitesurfers). Late summer and autumn bring the warmest sea.

Alexandria and the Mediterranean

The most temperate climate in the country, with the sea's humidity. Winter brings some rain and wind. Summer is busy with Egyptian holidaymakers.

The Western Desert

Hot by day for much of the year and very hot in summer. In winter, nights in the desert are cold, sometimes near freezing. Pack accordingly.

Through the year

  • December to February. Peak season in the Nile Valley: pleasant days, cool evenings and chilly early mornings. Busiest around Christmas and New Year. The Abu Simbel sun festival falls on or around 22 February.
  • March and April. Warming up, and still excellent for sightseeing. Spring winds can occasionally bring sand.
  • May to September. Hot to very hot in the south. It's the low season for Nile Valley sightseeing, so plan early starts and midday rests. The Red Sea is in full summer season.
  • October and November. Temperatures fall and the season restarts. It's an excellent time everywhere. The second Abu Simbel sun festival falls on or around 22 October.

Ramadan and holidays

Ramadan follows the Islamic lunar calendar and moves earlier each year. During the month:

  • opening hours of sites and shops often change;
  • many people fast from dawn to sunset;
  • evenings become especially lively and festive.

Travelling during Ramadan is entirely possible and can be memorable. Be discreet about eating and drinking in public during the day, and check opening times.

The Eid holidays after Ramadan and after the Hajj are busy domestic travel periods, particularly on the coasts.

How to choose

  1. If temples are your priority, travel between October and April, and book early for the Christmas and New Year peak.
  2. If you can only travel in summer, combine early-morning sightseeing with the Red Sea.
  3. If you want the desert, pick spring or autumn for the mildest days and nights.

Frequently asked questions

What is the worst time to visit Egypt?

For Nile Valley sightseeing, the height of summer, when the south is extremely hot. It's still possible with early starts, and it's the quietest season at the sites.

Is it cold in Egypt in winter?

Days are usually mild to warm, but mornings, evenings and desert nights can be cold. Bring layers.

Is it a good idea to travel during Ramadan?

Yes, with some planning: opening hours change, but the evenings are festive and welcoming.
