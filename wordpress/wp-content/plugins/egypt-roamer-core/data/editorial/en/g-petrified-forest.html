At the edge of Maadi, in Cairo, the ground is scattered with tree trunks that have turned to stone. The Petrified Forest is one of Egypt's smallest protected areas, and one of the closest to the city. This guide is based on the facts given by Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency, which manages the country's protected areas.

The protected area in brief

  • Declared: 1989.
  • Area: 3.3 km².
  • Type: natural monument.
  • Where: Maadi, Cairo Governorate, about 30 km from Cairo.

What you see

The area is dense with petrified stems and trunks of trees, part of the Gebel El-Khashab formation, which belongs to the Oligocene. The formation is made of layers of sand, gravel, clay and petrified wood, 70 to 100 m thick.

The remains of huge petrified trees take the shape of rock pieces with round cross-sections, from a few centimetres to several metres in size. Gathered together, they look like a forest of stone.

How the trees got here

The Agency explains that the trees probably did not grow where they lie. An ancient branch of the Nile, in remote geological times, most likely carried them over long distances and left them in this place, where they turned to stone.

How to visit

  • From Cairo: about 30 km away, according to the Agency.
  • Protect the place: never take pieces of petrified wood, and leave no rubbish.
  • Entry fees and rules: they can change, so check with the Environmental Affairs Agency before you go.

Bring the basics

There is little shade. Bring water, sun protection and good shoes.
