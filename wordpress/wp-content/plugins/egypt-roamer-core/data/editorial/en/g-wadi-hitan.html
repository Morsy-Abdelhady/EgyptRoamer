In the desert of the Fayoum, west of the Nile, the fossil skeletons of ancient whales lie in the open sand, with the shells and shark teeth of a long-vanished sea around them. Wadi Al-Hitan, Whale Valley, is Egypt's natural World Heritage site, and one of the strangest landscapes in the country. This guide explains what you will see and how to get there.

What Wadi Al-Hitan is

The valley holds fossils of the earliest whales, the Archaeoceti, a suborder that is now extinct. They tell one of the great stories of evolution: how the whale, once a land animal, became a mammal of the open ocean. UNESCO calls it the most important site in the world for showing this stage, and inscribed it as a World Heritage site in 2005.

The fossils here belong to the youngest of these early whales, in the last stages of losing their hind legs. Their number, their concentration and their quality are unique, and so is how easy they are to see in an open, protected landscape.

According to Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency, the fossils are about 40 million years old. Beside the whales lie shark teeth, shells and other sea creatures, and fossilised mangrove in the soft rock, which make the valley an open-air museum.

Part of a larger protected area

Wadi Al-Hitan lies in the northwest of the Wadi El-Rayan Protected Area, declared in 1989. The protected area also includes:

  • The lakes and waterfalls of Wadi El-Rayan, formed by drainage water from the farmland of the Fayoum.
  • Ain El-Rayan: moving sand dunes with four natural sulphur springs.
  • Gebel El-Rayan: deep gorges and a favourite viewpoint over Wadi El-Rayan.

The area is a refuge for threatened animals, among them the slender-horned and Dorcas gazelles, the fennec fox and the sand fox, and for rare migratory birds of prey.

How to visit

  • From Cairo: the trip takes a full day by 4×4, best with a specialist guide.
  • On foot inside the site: footpaths lead visitors to the main fossils, and vehicles are kept out of the site itself.
  • Protect the fossils: stay on the paths and never touch or take anything.
  • Entry and permits: rules can change, so ask your operator what the trip includes before you book.

Bring the basics

There is little shade in the open desert. Bring water, sun protection and good shoes.

Frequently asked questions

Why is it called Whale Valley?

Because of the fossil skeletons of early whales found there. Wadi Al-Hitan means "valley of the whales" in Arabic.

How old are the whale fossils?

About 40 million years old, according to Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency.

Is Wadi Al-Hitan a World Heritage site?

Yes. UNESCO inscribed it in 2005, as Egypt's natural World Heritage site.

Can I visit Wadi Al-Hitan in a day from Cairo?

Yes, as a full day trip by 4×4, best with a specialist guide.
