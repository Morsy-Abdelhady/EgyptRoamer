Luxor stands on the site of ancient Thebes. The ancient Egyptians built their great temples for the living on the East Bank, where the sun rises, and buried their kings on the West Bank. This guide covers the two great East Bank temples, Karnak and Luxor Temple, and how to see them.

Karnak

Karnak is one of the largest temple complexes in the world. At its heart is the great temple of the state god Amun-Ra, flanked by the precincts of the goddess Mut and the war god Montu. Built and rebuilt over many centuries, it has no single plan. It has been called a great historical document in stone.

The hypostyle hall

Karnak's most striking feature is the hypostyle hall, built between the second and third pylons and decorated under Seti I and Ramesses II. Twelve enormous columns, about 24 metres high, line the central nave, and with the side aisles the hall holds 134 columns. Reliefs on its outer walls show the victories of Seti I, and Ramesses II at the Battle of Kadesh.

At Karnak

Go early in the morning. The complex is huge and exposed, with little shade.

Luxor Temple

In the centre of the modern town, Luxor Temple was dedicated to Amun, his consort Mut and their son Khonsu. It was commissioned by Amenhotep III, completed under Tutankhamun and Horemheb, and enlarged by Ramesses II, who added a court, a pylon and obelisks. Its most striking feature is a colonnade of 14 columns, about 16 metres high.

The avenue of sphinxes

In ancient times, an avenue of sphinxes linked Luxor Temple with the temple of Amun at Karnak. Walking part of it between the two temples connects the visit.

Planning the day

  • Morning: Karnak, before the heat.
  • Midday: rest, or the Luxor Museum.
  • Dusk: Luxor Temple, beautifully lit as the light fades.

Frequently asked questions

Karnak or Luxor Temple, if I only have time for one?

Karnak, for its scale and the hypostyle hall. Luxor Temple is easier to fit in at dusk.

Are the East Bank temples part of a World Heritage site?

Yes. Karnak and Luxor Temple belong to Ancient Thebes with its Necropolis, inscribed by UNESCO in 1979.

Do I need a guide?

It's not essential, but a guide helps you read the reliefs and find your way around Karnak's huge complex.
