A night in the White Desert is one of the most memorable things to do in Egypt, and one of the least like the rest of the trip. There are no temples, just wind-carved chalk, a campfire and a sky full of stars. This guide explains what the trip is really like, so you know what you're signing up for.

The route

Most trips start from Cairo and head to Bahariya Oasis, the gateway to the desert, where you switch to a 4×4. From there the route crosses:

  • The Black Desert: sand dotted with cone-shaped hills capped in black volcanic stone.
  • Crystal Mountain: a ridge of quartz crystals by the roadside.
  • Agabat: valleys and dunes between the two deserts.
  • The White Desert: chalk shaped by the wind into giant mushrooms and pillars, protected as the White Desert Protected Area.

Sunset, night and sunrise

The formations are at their best when the light is low. Most trips arrive in the afternoon, so you see the chalk turn gold and pink at sunset. Camp is set up in the shelter of the rocks, dinner is cooked over a fire, and you sleep under blankets or in a simple tent. Morning brings a second show at sunrise.

It gets cold

Desert nights are much colder than most visitors expect, especially in winter. Bring warm layers, a hat and something to sleep in.

Camp life

Camps are basic, and that's part of the point. There are usually no toilets or showers. Ask your operator exactly what bedding, tents and food they provide, and whether there is a private tent or a shared shelter.

What to pack

  • Warm layers, a hat and a torch for the night.
  • Sun protection and sunglasses for the day.
  • A scarf or buff against wind and sand.
  • Wet wipes, basic medicine and any snacks you rely on.

Choosing an operator

  • Go with a licensed local operator and an experienced desert driver. Never drive into the desert on your own.
  • Check whether transfers from Cairo are included, or whether the trip starts in Bahariya.
  • Ask how many travellers share a vehicle and a camp.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do the White Desert as a day trip?

Not really. The distances are long, and the formations are at their best at sunset and sunrise. The overnight camp is the point.

When is the best season?

October to April, avoiding the extreme summer heat. Mid-winter nights are cold.

Is it suitable for children?

For children who are happy with basic camping, yes. Talk to the operator about bedding and warm clothing.
