The Citadel of Qaitbay stands on the Pharos peninsula in Alexandria, over the ruins of the ancient Lighthouse of Alexandria. Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities describes it as one of the most important forts on the Mediterranean coast. This guide explains what you will see.

Built on the Lighthouse

The Mamluk sultan al-Ashraf Abu al-Nasr Qaitbay built the citadel between 1477 and 1479 AD (882–884 AH), over the ruins of Alexandria's Lighthouse. It was Alexandria's defence against attacks from the sea.

Because of its strategic position, the citadel drew the attention of rulers through the ages. It was enlarged and conserved in 2003.

What you'll see

The citadel is built of limestone and covers about 17,550 square metres.

  • The outer wall: set with defensive towers, it surrounds the whole building.
  • The inner wall: it surrounds the courtyard and contains rooms, some used as barracks and others for storage.
  • The main tower: in the north-west of the courtyard, a massive square fort on three floors. Half-round towers at its four corners carry projecting balconies for defending the city.
  • The water tank: near the tower, it supplied the soldiers and their horses with water.

The visitor centre

Inside one of the halls of the main tower, a visitor centre set up with the French Centre for Alexandrian Studies explains the area. Nine information panels in Arabic and English, with maps and diagrams, cover the Royal Port, the Timonium building, the island of Antirhodos, the sea docks and the shipyards. They also tell the story of Alexandria's defences, from the role of Qaitbay to the forts Muhammad Ali Pasha built along the north coast. The ruins under the sea

Just off the citadel, at the eastern tip of the Pharos peninsula, lies a sunken archaeological site. It starts at the edge of the citadel and runs for 150 metres, at a depth of 6 to 8 metres. Work there began in 1994.

The site holds debris from the Lighthouse and its island: the heaviest blocks, over 20 tons, lie in a line heading north, which shows they fell from a tall structure. It also holds pieces that once stood around Pompey's Pillar, thrown into the harbour in 1167, under Saladin, to block a Crusader attack from Cyprus. More than 3,000 architectural pieces have been found (columns, statues, bases and capitals), dating from the 3rd century BC to the 7th century AD, along with the remains of three shipwrecks. Thirty-six large pieces are displayed at the Roman Theatre of Kom el-Dikka.

Frequently asked questions

Who built the Citadel of Qaitbay?

The Mamluk sultan al-Ashraf Abu al-Nasr Qaitbay, between 1477 and 1479 AD.

Is the Citadel of Qaitbay built on the Lighthouse of Alexandria?

Yes. It was built over the ruins of Alexandria's ancient Lighthouse, and debris from the Lighthouse lies on the seabed just off the citadel.

What is inside the Citadel of Qaitbay?

A courtyard surrounded by walls with barracks and storerooms, the three-floor main tower with its corner towers and balconies, a water tank and a visitor centre on the history of the site.

Can you see the remains of the Lighthouse?

The remains lie underwater, 6 to 8 metres deep, just off the citadel. Thirty-six large pieces from the site are displayed at the Roman Theatre of Kom el-Dikka.
