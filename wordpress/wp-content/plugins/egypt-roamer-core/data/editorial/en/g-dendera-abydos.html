North of Luxor, two temples show ancient Egyptian art at its most refined: the Temple of Hathor at Dendera and the Temple of Seti I at Abydos. They are less visited than Karnak or the Valley of the Kings, which is part of their charm. This guide covers what you will see and how to plan the trip.

Dendera: the Temple of Hathor

Dendera, on the west bank of the Nile in the Qena governorate, was the capital of a province of Upper Egypt and the city of Hathor, goddess of the sky and of fertility. Britannica calls her temple one of the best preserved in Egypt. The building you see dates from the Ptolemaic period and was completed under the Roman emperor Tiberius, on foundations that go back at least to Khufu, the builder of the Great Pyramid.

  • The hypostyle hall: 18 columns topped with the face of Hathor, under a ceiling carved with astronomical scenes.
  • The roof: a staircase leads up to chapels of Osiris. In one of them a carved zodiac was found; it was removed in 1820, and the original is now in the Louvre in Paris, with a copy in its place.
  • The crypts: twelve underground chambers, decorated with reliefs.
  • Cleopatra: on the outer rear wall, a relief shows Cleopatra VII with her son Caesarion.
  • Around the temple: a sacred lake, a small temple of Isis, two birth houses (mammisi) and a Christian church.

Abydos: the Temple of Seti I

Abydos, in the low desert west of the Nile near the town of Al-Balyana, is one of the most important sites of ancient Egypt. It was a burial ground for the earliest kings, and it became the great centre of the cult of Osiris, where Egyptians wished to be buried close to the god's tomb.

The Temple of Seti I, begun by Seti I and continued by his son Ramesses II, is famous for the delicacy and beauty of its reliefs.

  • Seven sanctuaries: an unusual plan, with seven chapels for the king and the great gods, reached through two hypostyle halls.
  • The Abydos King List: in a long gallery, Seti and Ramesses make offerings to the names of 76 earlier kings, beginning with Menes.
  • The Osireion: just behind the temple, an underground hall with ten stone pillars surrounded by a channel of water, thought to be Seti's cenotaph. Part of it is flooded.
  • The "helicopter": a famous carving that looks like a modern machine is really an inscription of Seti I that was plastered over and re-carved under Ramesses II; as the plaster fell away, the two texts mixed.

Planning the day

  • One long day: the two sites are often combined in a single day from Luxor, by car with a driver or on a guided tour. Ask what the tour includes before you book.
  • A guide: the stories in the reliefs come alive with an Egyptologist.
  • Dendera's roof and crypts: bring a torch for the darker rooms.
  • Travel advice: check the current advice for road travel in the area before you go.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Dendera zodiac original?

No. The zodiac on the roof chapel is a copy: the original was removed in 1820 and is in the Louvre in Paris.

What is the Abydos King List?

A relief in the Temple of Seti I showing Seti and his son Ramesses II honouring 76 earlier kings by name, beginning with Menes.

Can I see Dendera and Abydos in one day?

Many visitors do, in a long day from Luxor with a car and driver or on a tour.
