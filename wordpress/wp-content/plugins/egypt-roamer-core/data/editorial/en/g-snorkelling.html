Choosing between Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh for snorkelling comes down to the kind of day you want: island stops and an easy combination with Luxor, or reefs around Ras Mohammed and the Straits of Tiran. This guide compares boat and shore options, what beginners can ask an operator, and how to choose without assuming every trip is the same. For dive-site context, see our guide to the Red Sea's famous dive sites.

Hurghada: islands and easy boat trips

From Hurghada, the classic trip is a boat to the Giftun Islands, protected islands offshore with sandy beaches and fringing reefs. Many reefs are shallow enough to be excellent with just a mask and fins. Hurghada is also the Red Sea resort that combines most easily with Luxor and the Nile Valley.

Sharm El Sheikh: Ras Mohammed and Tiran

From Sharm El Sheikh, a well-known option is Ras Mohammed National Park, declared in 1983 and known for its coral reefs (more on it below). The reefs of the Straits of Tiran are another day-boat option; conditions vary by site, so ask the operator about that day's route and sea conditions. See our Ras Mohammed snorkelling trip.

When to snorkel

You can snorkel in the Red Sea all year, and the right time depends on what you want.

  • Late summer and autumn bring the warmest sea.
  • Spring and autumn give warm water without the summer heat on land, which makes them the most comfortable time for a whole day on a boat.
  • Winter is milder on land, with cooler water and more wind. Ask the operator about a wetsuit or rash vest for your dates.
  • On any day, wind and waves decide which reefs a boat can reach, so operators may change the route. Ask about that day's sea conditions before you book.

The best snorkelling spots

The best-known reefs lie in protected areas run by Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency (EEAA), so park rules apply on every trip.

  • Ras Mohammed National Park (Sharm El Sheikh): declared in 1983, about 778 km², where the Gulf of Suez meets the Gulf of Aqaba. Coral reefs surround it on all its sea sides; its eastern edge is a rock wall dropping into the gulf, and a mangrove channel about 250 m long separates the Ras Mohammed peninsula from Al-Baira island. The EEAA notes its colourful fish and endangered sea turtles.
  • Nabq Protected Area (South Sinai): declared in 1992, about 518 km², with coral reefs, dense mangrove forests, and desert and mountain landscapes; the EEAA names diving, safari and birdwatching among its attractions.
  • Abu Galum Protected Area (South Sinai): declared in 1992, about 426 km², where the mountains come close to the shore, with coral reefs, seagrass and lagoons. Its underwater cave system reaches depths of more than 100 m and, in the EEAA's words, is unstable and extremely dangerous: snorkel only where your guide takes you.
  • The Giftun Islands (Hurghada): protected islands offshore with sandy beaches and fringing reefs, the classic boat trip from Hurghada.
  • Wadi El-Gemal National Park (Marsa Alam): further south, with some of Egypt's best untouched reefs. See our Wadi El-Gemal guide.

Boat or shore

  • Boat trips reach the best offshore reefs and usually stop at two or three sites, often with lunch on board.
  • Shore snorkelling is possible from some resorts' house reefs and, at Ras Mohammed, from the beach on road trips.
  • Snorkellers and divers often share the same boat, which suits mixed groups. For diving, see our Red Sea diving trip.

For beginners

You don't need to be a strong swimmer. Most boats provide life jackets or snorkel vests, and guides accompany beginners. Tell the operator your level when you book, and start at a calm, shallow site.

Reef etiquette

  • Never touch, stand on or collect coral or shells.
  • Don't feed fish.
  • Use reef-friendly sun protection, or better, a rash vest.
  • Choose operators who use fixed moorings instead of anchoring on the reef.

How to choose

  • Choose Hurghada if you want easy island days and plan to combine the sea with Luxor.
  • Choose Sharm El Sheikh if the reefs are the main reason for your trip, or if you also want Mount Sinai.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time to snorkel in Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh?

Late summer and autumn bring the warmest sea, and spring and autumn are the most comfortable for a day on a boat. You can snorkel all year: in winter the water is cooler and the wind stronger, so ask about a wetsuit.

Where is the best snorkelling in the Red Sea?

The best-known reefs are in protected areas: Ras Mohammed, Nabq and Abu Galum near Sharm El Sheikh, the Giftun Islands off Hurghada, and Wadi El-Gemal near Marsa Alam.

Do I need my own equipment?

No. Masks, snorkels and fins are usually provided. A mask that fits you well makes a difference, so bring yours if you have one.

Will I see turtles or dolphins?

Turtles and rays are seen on many trips, but encounters with any animal depend on the site and the season, and nothing is guaranteed.
