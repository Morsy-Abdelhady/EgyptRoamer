The Red Sea is one of the easiest places in the world to see a coral reef: clear, warm water and reefs a short boat ride from the coast. Egypt's two big resort areas, Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh, both offer excellent snorkelling. This guide compares them and explains how to choose a trip.

Hurghada: islands and easy boat trips

From Hurghada, the classic trip is a boat to the Giftun Islands, protected islands offshore with sandy beaches and fringing reefs. Many reefs are shallow enough to be excellent with just a mask and fins. Hurghada is also the Red Sea resort that combines most easily with Luxor and the Nile Valley.

Sharm El Sheikh: Ras Mohammed and Tiran

From Sharm El Sheikh, the highlight is Ras Mohammed National Park, declared in 1983 as Egypt's first national park, with coral walls close to the surface. The reefs of the Straits of Tiran are another classic day-boat trip, with stronger currents. See our Ras Mohammed snorkelling trip.

Boat or shore

  • Boat trips reach the best offshore reefs and usually stop at two or three sites, often with lunch on board.
  • Shore snorkelling is possible from some resorts' house reefs and, at Ras Mohammed, from the beach on road trips.
  • Snorkellers and divers often share the same boat, which suits mixed groups. For diving, see our Red Sea diving trip.

For beginners

You don't need to be a strong swimmer. Most boats provide life jackets or snorkel vests, and guides accompany beginners. Tell the operator your level when you book, and start at a calm, shallow site.

Reef etiquette

  • Never touch, stand on or collect coral or shells.
  • Don't feed fish.
  • Use reef-friendly sun protection, or better, a rash vest.
  • Choose operators who use fixed moorings instead of anchoring on the reef.

How to choose

  • Choose Hurghada if you want easy island days and plan to combine the sea with Luxor.
  • Choose Sharm El Sheikh if the reefs are the main reason for your trip, or if you also want Mount Sinai.

Frequently asked questions

Can I snorkel all year?

Yes. The water is warmest from late summer into autumn and coolest in late winter, when a wetsuit helps.

Do I need my own equipment?

No. Masks, snorkels and fins are usually provided. A mask that fits you well makes a difference, so bring yours if you have one.

Will I see turtles or dolphins?

Turtles and rays are seen on many trips, but encounters with any animal depend on the site and the season, and nothing is guaranteed.
