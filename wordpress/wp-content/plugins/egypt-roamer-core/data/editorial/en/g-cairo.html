Most visitors see Cairo through the pyramids and the new museum at Giza, then leave. This guide is about the rest of the city: the neighbourhoods where Cairenes live, pray, shop and eat. It's where Cairo stops being a transit stop and starts being one of the great cities of the world. For the essentials (the sights, where to stay, how many days), start with our Cairo destination guide.

A walk through the old city

This route follows the spine of Fatimid Cairo from north to south. It's best in the late afternoon, when the light softens and the streets fill up:

  1. Start at Bab al-Futuh, one of the northern gates of the medieval city.
  2. Walk south along Al-Muizz Street, past mosques, madrasas and sabil-kuttabs (fountain-schools), some of them restored.
  3. Stop at the Qalawun complex, one of the great Mamluk monuments.
  4. Cross into the lanes of Khan el-Khalili, and take tea at an old café.
  5. Cross the main road to Al-Azhar Mosque.
  6. Continue south through the textile and spice streets to Bab Zuweila, and climb its minarets if they're open.
  7. End with sunset at Al-Azhar Park on the hill above the old city.

Walking the old city

Wear shoes with grip, since the streets are uneven. Dress modestly. Keep small notes for tea, entry to monuments and tips. Mosques ask visitors to remove their shoes, and women to cover their hair.

Neighbourhoods

Coptic Cairo

A walled, quiet quarter of churches, the Coptic Museum and the Ben Ezra Synagogue, inside the old Roman fortress of Babylon. Mar Girgis metro station is right beside it.

Downtown (Wust el-Balad)

The 19th-century centre, laid out in a European style, with grand façades, old cinemas, bookshops, galleries and local cafés. Walk it at night, when it's busiest.

Zamalek

The island neighbourhood of Gezira, leafy and cosmopolitan, full of galleries, restaurants and embassies. The Cairo Tower offers a view over the whole city.

Garden City

Curving streets of old villas and embassies along the Nile, just south of Downtown.

The Citadel and around

The hilltop fortress, the great mosques of Sultan Hassan and Al-Rifa'i below it, and, a short walk away, the Ibn Tulun mosque and the Gayer-Anderson Museum.

Everyday Cairo

  • The ahwa. The traditional coffee house is the city's living room: tea, coffee, shisha, backgammon and dominoes. Sitting in one is a rite of passage.
  • Eating late. Dinner is often late, and many places stay busy past midnight.
  • Friday. The weekly day of rest. Mornings are quiet, and mosques are busy around the midday prayer.
  • Traffic. Plan your days by area. Crossing the city at rush hour can take a long time.

On the river

End a day with a felucca at sunset from the Corniche, or a dinner cruise with music and a show. For a street-food evening with a local guide, see our night food tour.

Frequently asked questions

Is Islamic Cairo safe to walk around?

It's a busy, lived-in area, and walking in daytime and early evening is the normal way to explore it. Use ordinary city precautions with bags and phones, and dress modestly.

How long does the old-city walk take?

Allow a half-day at a relaxed pace, with stops for monuments and tea.

What's the best area for an evening out?

Downtown and Zamalek for cafés and restaurants, and the old city around Khan el-Khalili and Al-Muizz Street for atmosphere.
