In the south of Egypt's Red Sea coast, a national park joins the mountains of the Eastern Desert to the reefs offshore. Wadi El-Gemal National Park protects a great desert valley, a coast of mangroves and turtle beaches, and some of the best untouched coral reefs in Egypt. This guide explains what it protects and how to visit with care.

What the park protects

Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency declared Wadi El-Gemal a national park in 2003. It covers about 7,637 km² in the south of the Red Sea governorate: about 60 km of coast, reaching on average about 50 km inland into the mountains of the Eastern Desert and about 10 km out to sea.

From the mountains to the sea

  • Wadi El-Gemal: one of the largest and richest valleys of the Eastern Desert, where flourishing plant life supports many creatures, some of them rare and endangered.
  • Gebel Hamata: one of the highest mountains of the Eastern Desert, home to a great variety of plants and animals.
  • Mangroves: stretches of the coast hold some of the largest and most important mangrove thickets in Egypt, a rare and very fragile habitat.
  • Coral reefs: some of the best reefs in Egypt, still in their natural state and famous around the world, along the shore and around submerged islands out at sea.
  • Seagrass meadows: among the most important on the Red Sea, vital for rare sea creatures and for fish to breed.
  • Islands and beaches: islands of international importance for nesting birds and sea turtles, and the most important sea turtle nesting beaches in Egypt.

How to visit

  • With a local operator: the park's reefs draw visitors from around the world. Go with a licensed local operator, and ask what the trip includes before you book.
  • Respect the reef: never touch or stand on coral.
  • Respect nesting beaches: keep away from turtle nests.
  • Leave no trace: take all your rubbish with you, in the desert as on the coast.

A protected area

Wadi El-Gemal is a national park. Its rules protect habitats that are rare in Egypt, so follow them.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Wadi El-Gemal National Park?

In the south of Egypt's Red Sea governorate, between the mountains of the Eastern Desert and the sea.

When was Wadi El-Gemal declared a protected area?

In 2003, as a national park.

What can I see in Wadi El-Gemal?

Desert valleys and mountains, mangroves, coral reefs, seagrass meadows, and beaches and islands where sea turtles and birds nest.
