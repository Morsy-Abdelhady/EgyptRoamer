In South Sinai, the Taba Protected Area takes in a land of high plateaus, valleys and freshwater springs. This guide is based on the facts given by Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency, which manages the country's protected areas.

The protected area in brief

  • Declared: 1998.
  • Area: 2,977.8 km².
  • Type: natural monument.
  • Where: South Sinai Governorate, about 550 km from Cairo.

Landscape and heritage

The Agency describes the area's remarkable geology, archaeological sites up to about 5,000 years old, rare wildlife, beautiful scenery and the traditional heritage of the Bedouin who live there.

Plateaus rise to more than 1,000 m. Their rocks are crossed by many faults and joints, which give shelter to plants and animals.

The springs

Among the beauties of the area are its freshwater springs, where the water flows out over the ground. The Agency names:

  • Ain Hadra, in Wadi Ghazala.
  • Ain Umm Ahmed, in Wadi al-Sawana.
  • Ain Furtaga, in Wadi Watir.

According to the Agency, they can be visited easily.

Wildlife

  • Plants: the valleys hold important plant communities, such as acacia trees. 72 plant species have been collected in Wadi Watir, and the neighbouring areas have up to 480.
  • Mammals: among them the rock hyrax, the Nubian ibex, the wolf, the hyena and gazelles.
  • Birds: some valleys are a home for large birds such as the houbara bustard, and on the mountain tops live the Egyptian vulture, the bearded vulture and the golden eagle.

How to visit

  • Protect the place: never take plants or stones, keep the springs clean, and leave no rubbish.
  • Entry fees and rules: they can change, so check with the Environmental Affairs Agency or your operator before you go.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the Taba Protected Area?

In South Sinai Governorate, about 550 km from Cairo.

What springs are in the Taba Protected Area?

The Agency names Ain Hadra in Wadi Ghazala, Ain Umm Ahmed in Wadi al-Sawana and Ain Furtaga in Wadi Watir.

What animals live in the Taba Protected Area?

Among others, the rock hyrax, the Nubian ibex, the wolf, the hyena and gazelles, and birds such as the houbara bustard, the Egyptian vulture and the golden eagle.

How old are the archaeological sites in Taba?

Up to about 5,000 years old, according to the Environmental Affairs Agency.
