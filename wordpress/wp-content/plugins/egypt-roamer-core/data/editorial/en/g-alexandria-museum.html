The Alexandria National Museum tells the story of Egypt's arts and crafts, from prehistory to the modern era, in a single building in the heart of Alexandria. It is a good first stop to understand the city before you see its sites. This guide explains what you will see.

A palace turned museum

The building was first a palace for Asaad Basili Pasha, one of Alexandria's wealthiest figures, who had it designed in the Italianate style. The Supreme Council of Antiquities bought the property in 1996 and converted it into an archaeological museum, which opened to the public in 2003.

What you'll see

The collection, according to Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities, covers the heritage of Egyptian arts and crafts from prehistory to the modern era. Its highlights include:

  • Sunken antiquities from Abu Qir Bay: a rare group of objects recovered by underwater excavations.
  • The coin hall: coins from different periods of history.
  • Islamic-era weapons: a distinctive display.
  • Metals, ceramics and glass from across the collection.
  • Gold, silver and jewellery that once belonged to the family of Muhammad Ali Pasha.

Before you visit

  • Photography: photos with a mobile phone are free of charge; no flash and no tripods.
  • Access: the Ministry lists a ramped entrance, an elevator, parking, toilets and a gift shop.
  • Bags: large bags and parcels over 40 × 40 cm go to the cloakroom.
  • Tickets and opening hours: these change, so check the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities' website before you go.

Frequently asked questions

What is in the Alexandria National Museum?

Egyptian arts and crafts from prehistory to the modern era, including sunken antiquities from Abu Qir Bay, a coin hall, Islamic-era weapons, and jewellery of Muhammad Ali Pasha's family.

When did the Alexandria National Museum open?

In 2003. The Supreme Council of Antiquities bought the building, a former palace, in 1996.

Whose palace was the museum?

Asaad Basili Pasha, one of Alexandria's wealthiest figures, who had it built in the Italianate style.

Can I take photos in the Alexandria National Museum?

The Ministry says photos with a mobile phone are free of charge, without flash and without tripods.
