Ras Mohammed is the protected tip of the Sinai Peninsula, where the Gulf of Suez and the Gulf of Aqaba meet. Coral reefs surround it on every side that faces the sea. This guide is based on the facts given by Egypt's Environmental Affairs Agency, which manages the country's protected areas.

The park in brief

  • Declared: 1983.
  • Area: 778.2 km².
  • Type: national park.
  • Where: South Sinai Governorate, about 446 km from Cairo.

The reefs

On the park's eastern edge, a rock wall drops into the gulf, where the coral reefs grow. According to the Agency, the reefs surround Ras Mohammed on all of its sea sides and form a unique structure that has shaped the natural life of the area. Earthquakes have formed underwater caves beneath the island.

The waters are known for colourful fish, endangered sea turtles and rare marine life.

The mangrove channel

A mangrove channel about 250 m long separates the Ras Mohammed peninsula from the island of Al-Baira.

Wildlife on land

The park is not only a sea reserve. The Agency lists:

  • The Nubian ibex in the mountain areas.
  • Small mammals, reptiles and insects, many of which appear only at night.
  • Birds such as herons and gulls.

How to visit

  • From Sharm El Sheikh: most visitors come on a snorkelling or diving trip by boat. See our Ras Mohammed snorkelling trip and our Red Sea diving trip.
  • Protect the reef: never touch, stand on or take coral or shells.
  • Entry fees and rules: they can change, so check with the Environmental Affairs Agency or your operator before you go.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Ras Mohammed?

At the southern tip of the Sinai Peninsula, in South Sinai, where the Gulf of Suez meets the Gulf of Aqaba.

When did Ras Mohammed become a protected area?

It was declared in 1983, as a national park.

What can you see at Ras Mohammed?

Coral reefs on every sea side, colourful fish, endangered sea turtles, a mangrove channel, and on land the Nubian ibex and birds such as herons and gulls.

Can I visit Ras Mohammed from Sharm El Sheikh?

Yes. Most visitors come from Sharm El Sheikh on a snorkelling or diving trip.
