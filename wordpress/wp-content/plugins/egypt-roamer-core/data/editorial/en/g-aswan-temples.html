Aswan is the gateway to the temples of Nubia. Some stand on islands in the Nile, some were moved stone by stone when Lake Nasser rose, and the greatest of them, Abu Simbel, lies far to the south. This guide sorts them by how easy they are to reach, so you can choose what fits your days.

In and around Aswan

Philae

The temple of the goddess Isis on its island is the classic Aswan visit. Like Abu Simbel, it was moved during the UNESCO rescue campaign, to the island of Agilkia. UNESCO describes Philae as the final bastion of ancient Egyptian religion. You reach it by a short boat ride.

Around the city

  • The Unfinished Obelisk: a giant obelisk abandoned in the granite quarry where it was cut. UNESCO notes that the quarries are a basis of our knowledge of ancient Egyptian quarrying.
  • Qubbet el-Hawa: rock tombs of the officials who ran Nubian affairs in the Old and Middle Kingdoms, on the hill above the west bank.
  • The Monastery of St Simeon: the well-preserved ruins of one of the biggest monasteries in Egypt, on the west bank.

North of Aswan: Kom Ombo and Edfu

On the way to Luxor stand two of the best temples of the late period:

  • Kom Ombo: a double temple of the Ptolemaic and Roman periods, dedicated to Sobek, the crocodile god, and Horus, the falcon-headed god.
  • Edfu: the Temple of Horus, one of the best-preserved temples in Egypt, built between 237 and 57 BC.

Nile cruises stop at both. By road, they fit into the transfer day between Aswan and Luxor. See our guide Nile cruise or hotels?

Kalabsha and the temples of the lake

The rescue campaign moved several more temples to new sites:

  • Kalabsha: the temple of Kalabsha, with the temple of Beit el-Wali and the Kiosk of Qertassi, was reassembled near the High Dam.
  • The lake temples: Wadi es-Sebua, Dakka and Maharraqa were rebuilt near the former site of Wadi es-Sebua, and Amada and Derr near the former site of Amada. They are usually seen on a Lake Nasser cruise.

Abu Simbel

The two rock temples of Ramesses II are the high point of any trip to the south. They were moved in the 1960s, about 60 metres above their original site, and on about 22 February and 22 October the rising sun reaches the statues in the sanctuary. Most visitors go on a day trip from Aswan. Read our guide Abu Simbel from Aswan, or see the Abu Simbel day trip.

How to fit them in

  • One day in Aswan: Philae, the Unfinished Obelisk and a felucca at sunset.
  • Two days: add Abu Simbel.
  • On the way to Luxor: Kom Ombo and Edfu.
  • More time: a Lake Nasser cruise for Kalabsha and the lake temples.

Frequently asked questions

Which Aswan temple should I see if I only have one day?

Philae, by boat. Add the Unfinished Obelisk and end with a felucca on the Nile.

Are the Nubian temples part of a World Heritage site?

Yes. They belong to Nubian Monuments from Abu Simbel to Philae, inscribed by UNESCO in 1979.

Can I see Kom Ombo and Edfu without a cruise?

Yes, on a road transfer between Aswan and Luxor with a driver or a tour. More about the city is in our Aswan guide.
